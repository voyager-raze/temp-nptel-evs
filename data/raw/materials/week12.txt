 Water Act, 1974 (Prevention and Control of
 Pollution)

v The Act was enacted in 1974 to implement the decision taken at the
  UN Conference on Human Environment, Stockholm, 1972.

v The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act was last
  amended in 2003.

v The main objectives of the Water Act are to provide for prevention,
  control and abatement of water pollution and the maintenance or
  restoration of the wholesomeness of water

v Central and State Pollution Control Boards were established to
  monitor and enforce the regulations, collect and analyse samples,
  impose penalties, close industrial units, publish names of offenders

v It applies to all wastewater discharges ­ municipal and industrial
   Air Act, 1981 (Prevention and Control of Pollution)

v The main objectives of the Act are:

(i) To provide for the prevention, control and abatement of air pollution.

(ii) To confer on Central and State Pollution Control Boards the powers to
implement the provisions of the Act and assign to the Boards functions
relating to pollution.
vThe Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act was enacted by the

  Parliament in 1981 and amended in 1987.
vAccording to this Act, no person can operate certain types of industries

  including asbestos, cement, fertilizer and petroleum industries without
  consent of the State Board.
vIt states that sources of air pollution such as industry, vehicles, power
  plants, etc., are not permitted to release particulate matter, lead, carbon
  monoxide, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxide, volatile organic compounds
  (VOCs) or other toxic substances beyond a prescribed level.
    Environment Protection Act, 1986

vThe Environment Protection Act was enacted in 1986 with the objective of
  providing protection and improvement of the environment.

vThe Act was last amended in 1991. It empowers the Central Government to
  establish authorities [under section 3(3)] charged with the mandate of
  preventing environmental pollution in all its forms and to tackle specific
  environmental problems that are peculiar to different parts of the country.

vAccording to this act `Environment' includes (i) water, air and land, and (ii) the
  interrelationship which exists among and between (a) water, air and land and
  (b) human beings, other living creatures, plants, micro-organisms and
  property.

v Environmental Impact Assessments became mandatory for all large projects
  due to this Act.
      Hazardous Waste (Management, Handling and
          Transboundary Movement) Rules, 1989

v These Rules came into effect in the year 1989 and have been amended in the years
   2000, 2003 and 2022. The current notification of the Hazardous Waste (Management,
   Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2022 supersedes former notifications.

v Hazardous waste means any waste which by reason of any of its physical, chemical,
   reactive, toxic, flammable, explosive or corrosive characteristics causes danger or is
   likely to cause danger to health or environment, whether alone or when in contact
   with other wastes or substances.

v Hazardous Waste Management Rules are notified to ensure safe handling, generation,
   processing, treatment, packaging, storage, transportation, use, reprocessing,
   collection, conversion, and offering hazardous waste for sale, destruction and
   disposal.

v The Rules lay down corresponding duties of various authorities such as MoEFCC,
   CPCB, State/UT Govts., SPCBs/PCCs, DGFT, Port Authority and Custom Authority while
   State Pollution Control Boards/Pollution Control Committees have been designated
   with wider responsibilities touching almost every aspect of hazardous wastes
   generation, handling and their disposal.

v Waste rubber tyres are now under the Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) as of
   2022

   Principles and doctrines vis-à-vis Environment

§ The Doctrine of Absolute Liability
     § The owners of the industry or authorities are fully liable for granting
        compensation to the victims of any environmental disaster due to them and
        have to pay for restoration of the environment

§ Polluter Pays Principle
     § The polluter is fully liable for the cost of remediating pollution resulting
        from their past or present activities

§ Precautionary Principle
     § The judges may examine a case on the basis of potential threat to the
        environment or human health; burden of proof lies with the proposer

§ The Doctrine of Sustainable Development
     § Sustainable development for improving the quality of life, eliminating
        poverty and ensuring that natural assets are not exhausted in one
        generation

§ The Public Trust Doctrine
     § The State is the trustee of the `Commons' that include air, water, sea and
        forests and these cannot be subject to private ownership and have to be
        protected for public use.
  Landmark Judgments related to the Environment

· Municipal Council, Ratlam vs. Shri Vardhichand & Ors.; Supreme Court of
   India (date of judgment 29 July 1980)

· The residents of Ratlam municipality were frustrated about the lack of
   sanitary facilities and the contamination in the streets. They brought suit
   against the municipality under Section 133 of the Criminal Procedure Code
   for public nuisance.

· Ratlam municipality's obligations to its people under Section 123 M. P.
   Municipalities Act of 1961, include the provision of sanitary facilities and the
   prevention of street contamination from a nearby alcohol plant.

· The municipality argued in its defense that 1) the residents chose to live
   where there are no facilities, and 2) the authorities lacked funds necessary to
   construct the facilities required to comply.

· Supreme Court decision was that a municipality could not claim inability to
   provide basic sanitation facilities and prevent street contamination due to
   lack of financial ability.

· The municipality was ordered to take immediate action with regards to the
   contamination and pollution in the streets. It was also ordered to provide
   sanitary facilities and services for its residents within a six-month period.

· M.C. Mehta and others vs. Union Of India and Ors. Etc 1986 in which the SCR
