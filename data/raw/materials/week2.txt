What is urbanization?

 Urbanization is a complex socio-economic process that transforms the built

environment, converting formerly rural into urban settlements, while also

shifting the spatial distribution of a population from rural to urban areas. It

includes changes in dominant occupations, lifestyle, culture and behaviour,

and thus alters the demographic and social structure of both urban and

rural areas.           NPTEL

                                                                                 04
Benefits of urbanization

                                    Urban areas are the world's economic platforms for

                                           production, innovation and trade. They offer
                                           significant opportunities for both formal and
                                           informal employment, generating a sizeable share
                                           of new private sector jobs. Urbanization has helped
                                           millions escape poverty through increased

                               Lproductivity, employment opportunities, improved

                          NPT infrastructure and services.

Sources of urban growth

 Urban growth has three components: natural increase, migration and

reclassification. Their respective contributions to urban growth differ

depending on the demographic changes taking place in a country and the

size of a given settlement, on spatial planning policies and national

definitions of urban space, and on the physical environment and other

country-specific or local circumstances.  NPTEL

The urban heat island effect

 The urban heat island (UHI) effect is the increase in temperature of dense

     urban areas compared with their suburban or rural surroundings. The UHI
     arises through shifts in energy fluxes associated with land use change as
     well as increased anthropogenic heat from buildings and vehicles.
      A typical urban heat island profile, showing higher air temperature in

  NPTEL built-up areas and lower temperature in rural areas

       (Nature Reviews Earth & Environment, 2021, 2, 166­181)

                       NPTEL                           other infrastructure tend to reflect less

                                                       trees, vegetation, and other natural

Other environmental impacts of buildings

 Conventional buildings use large amounts of energy, land, water, and raw

materials for their construction and operation. They are responsible for

large greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions as well as emissions of other

harmful air pollutants. They also generate large amounts of construction

Sustainability in built environment: Green building

 Green building is the practice of (1) increasing the efficiency with which

     buildings and their sites use energy, water, and materials, and (2) reducing
     building impacts on human health and the environment, through better
     siting, design, construction, operation, maintenance, and removal.

Purpose of green building

 Green building involves a vast array of practices and a host of techniques

to reduce and ultimately eliminate the impacts of buildings on the

environment and human health. While such practices or technologies, are

constantly evolving and may differ from region to region, the overall

objective of green building is to optimize at least one or more of the

following principles:                      NPTEL

­ siting and structure design efficiency
­ energy efficiency
­ water efficiency

­ materials efficiency

­ improvement of indoor air quality

­ operations and maintenance optimization

­ waste and toxics reduction                                                 09
Siting and structure design efficiency

 Soil limitations before construction, such as slope and site drainage, depth

     to water table and site orientation for solar access (especially unimpeded
     southern exposure for winter season solar access) should be considered.

 The site should be within close proximity to basic services (grocery stores,

  libraries, post offices, cafes, medical facilities, hospitals and schools), which
  allow, even encourage, residents to walk within their neighborhoods to

  L access basic goods and services.
  TE Green sites effectively manage surface water from precipitation by
  NP capturing it on-site and using it for landscaping or greywater purposes.

 Building orientation should be such that it receives maximize free energy

     from the sun and wind, thus saving enormous amounts of energy and
     reduce carbon footprint.

Water efficiency

 Improved technology and fixture changes, such as low-flow fixtures on

     faucets and showerheads, can reduce the consumption of water per use.

 Bathrooms can be installed or retrofitted with low-flow or waterless urinals

  and toilets that use considerably less water for flushing.

  L Runoff from roads, paved areas, or other impervious surfaces can be
  E collected in cisterns, barrels, or swales, and thereafter detained, retained,
  T and routed for use in building evaporative coolers and toilets, and for
  NP irrigation purposes.
 Biological filtering techniques can be used to remove silt and pollutants

 Gray water, i.e., water drained from baths, showers, washing machines,

     and sinks, can be collected and reused for irrigating landscapes.

Materials efficiency

 Materials used should result in reduced maintenance and replacement

     costs over the life of the building, conserve energy, and improve occupant
     health and productivity.

 Materials should be selected after evaluating characteristics such as reused

  and recycled content, zero or low off-gassing of harmful air emissions, zero

  L or low toxicity, sustainably and rapidly renewable harvested materials, high
  E recyclability, durability, longevity, and local production.
  PT Building materials should be extracted and manufactured locally to the
  N building site to minimize the energy embedded in their transportation.

 Where possible, building elements should be manufactured off-site and

     delivered to site, to maximize benefits of off-site manufacture including
     minimizing waste, maximizing recycling, high quality elements, better
     occupational health and safety management, less noise and dust.

Improving indoor air quality

 Construction materials, interior finish products (such as cabinets, flooring,

     trim and counter tops), and cleaning/maintenance products should be
     chosen that emit zero or low levels of volatile organic compounds (VOCs).

 Buildings should be designed to provide adequate ventilation of air into

  them as well as filtration of this air to remove hazardous particles.

  EL Bathrooms should have manually operated ventilation systems that expel
  T moisture and humidity to avoid mold and mildew associated with wet and
  NP humid areas.
 Stomp off zones should be installed or simply stomp off mats should be

  used near entrances to keep outside dust and contaminants contained near

  entry ways where they can easily be removed rather than be distributed

  throughout the home.

 Compost bins can be provided to reduce the use of landfills.

 Demolition generates large amounts of materials that can be reused or
