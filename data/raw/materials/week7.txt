 A linear economy focuses on mining raw materials and processing them

     into a final product, while any waste from the production process and the
     product itself are thrown away after use. The circular economy model, on
     the other hand, recycles the wastes thrown away and reuse them in the
     production process.

Sustainability vs. Circularity

 Sustainability is focused on the long-term period and assumes that our

     actions bring environmental, economic, and social benefits without polluting
     the environment or spending too many resources.

  Striving for sustainable development, businesses need to adapt their way of

  L operating. This includes the implementation of circular value chains and the
  E reduction of their carbon footprint as part of a holistic sustainability strategy.
  NPT Circularity is about the practices which lead to sustainability.

 It aims to tackle climate change and other global challenges like biodiversity

     loss, waste, and pollution, by decoupling economic activity from the

     consumption of finite resources and maximizing the reuse of resources.

Limitations of the circular economy

 Thermodynamic limits: Cyclical systems consume resources and create

     wastes and emissions.

 System boundary limits: Spatial (problems are shifted along the product life

  cycle) and temporal (short term non-renewables use can build long-term

  renewable infrastructure).

  L Limits posed by physical scale of the economy: Rebound effect, boomerang
  E effect.
  NPT Limits posed by path-dependency and lock-in: First technologies retain their
  market position despite of in-efficiency.

                                                                                    10
Limitations of the circular economy

 Limits of governance and management: Intra-organizational and intra-

     sectoral management of inter-organizational and inter-sectoral physical
     flows of materials and energy.

 Limits of social and cultural definitions: The concept of waste has a strong

     influence on its handling, management and utilization The concept is

  L culturally and socially constructed The concept of waste is always
  TE constructed in a certain cultural, social and temporal context and this
  NP context is dynamic and changing.
