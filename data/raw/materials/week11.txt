  Ecology and ecosystems - definitions

Ecology

'eco' derived from the Greek word `oikos' meaning house or place to live. Ecology literally means the study of
  organisms `at home'.

Definition:

The study of the interrelationships between living organisms or groups of organisms (biotic) and their
  environment (biotic and abiotic)

Ecosystem

A self-contained community of organisms (or groups of organisms) and their non-living environment

Defined by flows of mass and energy in and out of the system and the impact on the abiotic (non-living) and
  biotic (living) components of the system; and some degree of functional stability.

Ecosphere or biosphere

The aggregation of all ecosystems on Earth

           ECOLOGICAL HIERARCHY

· Population refers to the numbers or groups of organisms of the same species
   that occupy a given area (or ecosystem or part of it). These populations, their
   interrelationships, and relationship with their environment are impacted by
   any physico-chemical changes within the ecosystem.

· Communities: Populations of different species thus affected constitute
   communities within an ecosystem. These communities (large or small-in terms
   of diversity or area) determine the flows of mass and energy in an ecosystem,
   and are themselves affected in terms of their dynamics, structure, growth and
   development, adaptation, and homeostasis.

· Homeostasis in ecology is the ability of an ecosystem to maintain its overall
   stability despite any disturbances. It is a result of the combination of
   biodiversity and the ecological interactions that occur between the species. -
   The ecosystem is maintained as an equilibrium.
                  ECOLOGICAL HIERARCHY

· Habitat: place or set of environmental conditions in which a specific organism lives
· Niche: Position occupied by an organism in an ecosystem ­ can be defined in an

   abstract, all-encompassing sense, or more specifically as:
     Spatial niche: physical space occupied by the organism, distribution of population,
     habitat
     Trophic niche: functional role in the community, trophic level, position in
     environmental gradients of pH, light, temperature, moisture, soil,.......as shown in
     next slide
     Behavioural niche: its way of life, predator-prey relationship with other organisms,
     competitor

Solar irradiation (outside the atmosphere)
     · Solar flux is a constant outside or above the atmosphere, ~ 1365 W/m2
     · Varies with place (Latitude) and time of day and season (next slide)
     · Energy is in the form of light and/or heat.; 1 Watt = 1 J/s
     · 99% of all spectral radiation falls between 136 and 4000 nm
     · Half of all radiation (47%) lies in the visible region ­ 380 to 770 nm
          · 7% in the UV and 46% in the IR region of solar spectrum

SOLAR RADIATION

Solar irradiation at sea level

     · A significant fraction of the solar flux is depleted as it passes through the
        atmosphere

          · Reflection: when reflected light has the same wavelength as incident
             light; by earth's surface (4%), clouds (20%) and atmosphere (6%) - total
             30%

          · Radiation: when radiated energy has a different wavelength compared
             to incident light ­ generally earth's radiation is long-wave and mainly
             in the form of heat; to space from clouds and atmosphere (64%) and
             earth (6%) ­ total 70%

          · Absorption: when incident light is absorbed, the temperature of the
             body rises ­ no output of energy is implied only input and
             accumulation of energy within the object/ body; by land and oceans
             (51%), clouds (3%) and atmosphere (16%), absorbed by atmosphere
             (15%) ­ eventually radiated out

Productivity: rate of fixation of energy into biomass

Gross primary productivity (GPP) is the total amount of energy consumed per
unit area per unit time (kcal/m2-yr); or
the total rate of photosynthesis including organic matter consumed for respiration
during the measurement period.

Net primary productivity (NPP) is gross productivity minus respiration.
Estimated based on the standing crop or biomass of photosynthetic organisms
only. Represents chemical energy in the biomass generated.

Respiration (R) represents energy consumed in metabolism and other activities
without generating biomass.

  (based on published literature) but not all species (Kormondy, 1996). It may or      GROWTH CURVE
  may not be the best-fit model in all cases or even for the same species.

· It applies only to those populations where the growth rate is maximum in the
  beginning and decreases monotonically over an extended period of time, i.e.,
  the population is growing exponentially at all times but with a decreasing
  growth rate. There is no time lag or lag phase in these populations. Note: The
  initial part of the `S' curve is not indicative of a lag phase, it is the nature of
  the logistic growth curve. This is the fastest growth phase.....

· For populations where the growth rate varies over time and non-
  monotonically, e.g., India, it cannot be applied. It applies to Indian data only
  from 1971 onwards.

  application' paper (Pearl and Reed, 1920), it was not an accurate prediction

BIODIVERSITY

      · Genetic: within a single species there can be thousands of strains or
         cultivars (cultivated or cultured strains).
           · Example: varieties of mangoes (1000s), 50,000 types of rice in India.

         of exchanging genes or interbreeding.

              Indian, Indonesian or south-east Asian and Congo.

      · Ecological: within a large country like India there are several types of
         ecosystems ­ deserts, alpine, mangroves, freshwater, seawater unlike
         smaller Scandinavian countries with fewer types of ecosystems

                     SOME DEFINITIONS

· Bioaccumulation
     · Increase in concentration of a chemical in the tissue of an
        organism over time by respiratory or dermal pathways or dietary
        ingestion

· Biomagnification
     · Increase in concentration of a chemical in the tissue of
        organisms at higher trophic levels

· Bioconcentration
     · Increase in concentration of a contaminant in the tissue of an
        organism by respiratory or dermal pathways or dietary ingestion
        with respect to water
     · Bioconcentration Factor (BCF) = Cbiota/Cwater
     · BCF has units of (mg of contaminant/kg of tissue)/mg of
        contaminant/L of water = L of water/kg of tissue

   Eutrophication

· Eutrophication: excessive growth of phytoplankton and plants due to
  high nutrient concentrations in a water body

· Eutrophication is a natural aging process that all lakes (and reservoirs)
  undergo

· Cultural eutrophication ® accelerated eutrophication of natural water
  bodies due to human activities

· All lakes accumulate silt and organic matter ® increase in nutrient
  levels ® increase in primary productivity ® increase in turbidity due to
  microbial growth (algae and bacteria)

· Oligotrophic lakes ® low nutrient levels ® low primary productivity
       Water quality and ecological impacts associated with

                                 eutrophication

· Noxious algal growth: leads to increase in scum, exudates that may
    be toxic, color, taste, odor, turbidity, and NOM levels
     · NOM leads to disinfection by-product formation during
         disinfection with halogenated compounds like chlorine

· Creates an anaerobic zone below the algal layer
      Bacterial growth and organic decomposition:
      ® low pH and DO levels
      ® loss of habitat for fish and fish food

· Excessive macrophyte (weeds) growth: loss of open water
· Excessive organic matter production due to algal and bacterial

    growth (smothers eggs and bugs)
· Cyanobacteria are inedible by some zooplankton (reduced food

    chain efficiency)
     · Toxic effects of algal blooms
· "Toxic" gases (ammonia, H2S) in bottom water (more loss of fish
    habitat)

                    PEDOGENESIS

Climate
     · Rainfall: leaching of minerals (loss) and wet deposition of particulate matter
        and minerals from atmosphere (gain)
     · Temperature: physical weathering ­ contraction and expansion; chemical
        weathering ­ acids in rainwater breakdown rock

Organisms

     · Microbes like bacteria, fungi, and algae and small animals like earthworms,
        snails, and insects inhabit the soil ecosystem and modify it chemically and
        biologically

     · Digestion of organic matter and breakdown of minerals by acids generated
        during digestion

Topography

     · Weathering at higher altitudes is greater than that at lower altitudes due to
        greater variations in climatic factors like temperature, and wind patterns.

     · Leeward versus windward sides of hills and mountains have different soil
        textures though the parent materials are the same
                          PEDOGENESIS

· Parent material
    · The D or R horizon is the original source of soil material.
      Weathering of this material results in generation of soil

· Time
    · Mature or older soils differ from young soils in terms of
      their retention of parent material and percentage of
      organic matter.
    · Young soils have more parent material and older soils
      have more organic matter

· Other processes
    · Calcification: removal of salts except Ca and Mg
    · Salinization: accumulation of salts due to evaporation in
      arid and semi-arid regions
    · Laterization: accumulation of Fe and Al in humid
      tropical environments

  Soil pollution control

· Phytoremediation
    · Some plants are hyperaccumulators of heavy metals and
      can grow in contaminated soils
    · Harvesting of these plants can be used to decontaminated
      such sites

· Bioremediation
    · Synthetic organic compounds can be degraded by
      bacteria, fungi and other organisms
        Improving soil quality

· Avoid continuous cultivation of same crop
· Land should lie fallow for some time and crops should be rotated

  (legumes and non-legumes)
· Soil moisture should be maintained by mulching of crop residue;

  compost or peat addition to improve soil quality
· Over-irrigation should be avoided to prevent salinization of soil
· Grow pest-resistant crop species
         Nutrient Cycling

· The 16 elements that plants cannot grow without
     · Macronutrients: C H N O P S Ca Mg K Cl
     · Micronutrients: Fe Mn Zn Cu B and Mo

· Carbon, hydrogen and oxygen in the atmosphere are plenty and are not growth
  limiting factors

· Growth limiting factors are mainly N, P and S
· Biogeochemical cycles are responsible for the availability of N, P and S

     · Soil is the most important environmental media for completion of these
        biogeochemical cycles

     · Soil degradation can lead to disturbance in nutrient cycling in the
        environment
