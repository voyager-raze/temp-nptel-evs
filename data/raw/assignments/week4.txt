QUESTION 1:

Climate is typically defined based on atmospheric observations averaged over what
minimum time period?

a. 1 year
b. 10 years
c. 30 years
d. 100 years.

Correct answer: c. 30 years

Solution: According to the World Meteorological Organization, climate is commonly
described using averages and variability of weather-related parameters over a period of
at least 30 years.

QUESTION 2:

Which of the following methods is commonly used to reconstruct past atmospheric
temperatures and climate conditions?

    a. By relying solely on historical records and written documents
    b. By measuring present-day ocean currents
    c. By analyzing tiny air bubbles preserved within ice cores
    d. By using satellite observations collected since the 1800s

Correct answer: c. By analyzing tiny air bubbles preserved within ice cores

Solution: Refer to Slide #13 of Lecture #1. Ice cores extracted from glaciers and ice sheets
contain tiny air bubbles that were trapped when the ice formed. These bubbles preserve
information about past atmospheric composition (e.g., CO2 and CH4 concentrations),
allowing scientists to reconstruct past climate conditions and estimate historical
temperatures over thousands of years.

QUESTION 3:

If global photosynthetic activity increased significantly while fossil fuel emissions
remained unchanged, how would the atmospheric CO2 record represented by the Keeling
Curve most likely respond?
    a. A reduced rate of increase in atmospheric CO2 concentration
    b. A steeper increase in atmospheric CO2 concentration
    c. Complete disappearance of seasonal CO2 fluctuations
    d. No measurable change in atmospheric CO2 concentration

Correct answer: a. A reduced rate of increase in atmospheric CO2 concentration
Solution: Photosynthesis removes CO2 from the atmosphere and stores it in plant
biomass. If global photosynthetic activity increased substantially, more CO2 would be
absorbed from the atmosphere, partially offsetting fossil fuel emissions. As a result, the
Keeling Curve would still show rising atmospheric CO2 concentrations, but the rate of
increase would be slower than before. Seasonal fluctuations would not disappear
because they are primarily driven by the seasonal growth and decay cycles of vegetation.

QUESTION 4:

Which of the following best describes the influence of anthropogenic aerosols on Earth's
climate?

    a. They contribute only to climate warming.
    b. They contribute only to climate cooling.
    c. They have no significant effect on climate.
    d. They can exert both warming and cooling effects on climate.

Correct answer: d. They can exert both warming and cooling effects on climate.
Solution: Refer to Slide #10 of Lecture #2. Different aerosols affect climate in different
ways. Sulfate aerosols generally cool the climate by reflecting incoming solar radiation,
whereas black carbon (soot) absorbs sunlight and contributes to warming. Aerosols can
also modify cloud properties, leading to additional cooling or warming effects.
Consequently, anthropogenic aerosols can have both warming and cooling influences on
the climate system.

QUESTION 5:

One carbon credit represents the offset of 1 tonne of CO2 equivalent (CO2e). A coal-fired
power plant emits 2.5 kg of CO2 per kWh of electricity generated and operates
continuously at a capacity of 500 MW throughout the year (365 days). How many carbon
credits would be required annually to offset 50% of the plant's CO2 emissions? How many
would be required to offset 100% of its emissions?

    a. 5,475,000 and 10,950,000
    b. 4,765,000 and 9,850,000
    c. 5,375,000 and 10,450,000
    d. 4,565,000 and 9,350,0004

Correct answer: a. 5,475,000 & 10,950,000
Solution: Annual electricity generation = 500 MW × 365 days × 24 h/day = 4,380,000
MWh = 4.38 × 109 kWh
Annual CO2 emissions = 2.5 kg/kWh × 4.38 × 109 kWh = 1.095 × 1010 kg = 1.095 × 107
tons
Since 1 carbon credit = 1 tonne CO2e:

    · Credits for 100% offset = 10,950,000
    · Credits for 50% offset = 10,950,000 / 2 = 5,475,000

QUESTION 6:

Which of the following is a reliable indicator of climate change that is directly linked to
rising ocean temperatures?

      a. Rise in global average sea levels
     b. Ocean acidification
      c. Destruction of coral reefs
      d. Increased volcanic activity on the ocean floor

Correct answer: a. Rise in global average sea levels
Solution: Refer to Slide #11 of Lecture #3. Rising ocean temperatures contribute directly
to sea level rise through two primary mechanisms: thermal expansion ­ as seawater
warms, it expands, increasing sea volume, and melting of glaciers and ice sheets ­
warmer oceans accelerate the melting of ice shelves and glaciers that feed into the sea.
As a result, the rise in global average sea levels is a direct and measurable indicator
of climate change, closely linked to increasing ocean temperatures.

QUESTION 7:

Which of the following is not generally classified as a geoengineering strategy for
mitigating climate change?

      a. Ocean fertilization
      b. Stratospheric aerosol injection
      c. Marine cloud brightening
      d. Deep ocean storage of CO2

Correct answer: d. Deep ocean storage of CO2
Solution: Refer to Slide #21 of Lecture #4. Geoengineering typically refers to large-scale
deliberate interventions in the Earth's climate system, such as ocean fertilization,
stratospheric aerosol injection, and marine cloud brightening. These are examples
of carbon dioxide removal (CDR) or solar radiation management (SRM) approaches. In
contrast, deep-ocean storage of CO2 is generally considered a carbon capture and
storage (CCS) technique rather than a geoengineering strategy.

QUESTION 8:

Permafrost thaw and subsidence will directly lead to
      a. addition of more greenhouse gases to the atmosphere
     b. increased storm surge levels
      c. ocean acidification
      d. proliferation of invasive species

Correct answer: a. addition of more greenhouse gases to the atmosphere

Solution: Refer to Slide #8 of Lecture #3. In the Arctic, large amounts of organic carbon
are stored in permafrost. The carbon in the permafrost has built up over thousands of
years, as dead plants have been buried and accumulated within layers of frozen soil,
where the cold prevents the organic material from decomposing. As the Arctic warms
and soils thaw, the organic matter in these soils will decompose rapidly and return to
the atmosphere as CO2 or CH4, both important greenhouse gases that further warm the
Arctic.

QUESTION 11:

Which of the following are major greenhouse gases that occur naturally in Earth's
atmosphere?

    a. Carbon dioxide (CO2)
    b. Methane (CH4)
    c. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
    d. Water vapor (H2O)

Correct answer(s):

    a. Carbon dioxide (CO2)
    b. Methane (CH4)
    d. Water vapor (H2O)
Solution: Carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and water vapor (H2O) are naturally
occurring greenhouse gases that help regulate Earth's temperature.
Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are synthetic compounds produced by human activities
and do not occur naturally in the atmosphere, although they are potent greenhouse
gases.

QUESTION 12:

Which of the following statements regarding the role of the ocean in Earth's climate
system are correct?

    a. The ocean has no significant influence on atmospheric CO2 concentrations.
    b. The ocean absorbs approximately one-quarter of anthropogenic CO2 emissions.
    c. Thermal expansion of seawater contributes to global sea-level rise.
    d. The ocean's large heat capacity and thermal inertia can delay surface warming

         over long timescales.
Correct answer(s):

    b. The ocean absorbs approximately one-quarter of anthropogenic CO2
         emissions.

    c. Thermal expansion of seawater contributes to global sea-level rise.
    d. The ocean's large heat capacity and thermal inertia can delay surface

         warming over long timescales.
Solution: The ocean is a major regulator of Earth's climate. It absorbs roughly 25% of
human-generated CO2 emissions, helping to moderate atmospheric CO2 levels. As
ocean water warms, it expands, contributing to sea-level rise. In addition, the ocean's
enormous heat capacity gives it substantial thermal inertia, allowing it to absorb and
store heat, which slows the rate of surface warming and causes climate responses to
unfold over decades to centuries. Therefore, statement a is incorrect.

QUESTION 13:

Which of the following factors can influence Earth's energy budget?

      a. Land-use changes
      b. Anthropogenic aerosols
      c. The extent of sea ice and seasonal snow cover
      d. The magnitude of ocean heat uptake

Correct answer(s):

      a. land-use changes
      b. anthropogenic aerosols
      c. extent of sea ice and seasonal snow cover
      d. magnitude of ocean heat uptake

Solution: Refer to Slide #9 of Lecture #1. All four factors affect Earth's energy balance.
Land-use changes alter surface albedo and energy fluxes. Anthropogenic aerosols
influence the absorption and reflection of solar radiation and can modify cloud properties.
Sea ice and snow cover affect planetary albedo, with greater coverage reflecting more
sunlight back to space. Ocean heat uptake regulates how much excess heat is stored in
the oceans, influencing the rate of atmospheric warming. Therefore, all the listed factors
contribute to Earth's energy budget.

QUESTION 14:

Which of the following are examples of cascading climate impacts?

    a. Melting ice reduces Earth's albedo, leading to additional warming and further ice
        loss.

    b. Sea-level rise increases the risk of storm-surge flooding, causing infrastructure
        damage and economic losses.

    c. Rising temperatures reduce crop yields, leading to higher food prices and adverse
        health outcomes.

    d. An isolated impact that remains confined to a single sector without triggering
         secondary effects.

Correct answer:

    a. Melting ice reduces Earth's albedo, leading to additional warming and further
         ice loss.

    b. Sea-level rise increases the risk of storm-surge flooding, causing
         infrastructure damage and economic losses.

    c. Rising temperatures reduce crop yields, leading to higher food prices and
         adverse health outcomes.

Solution: Cascading climate impacts occur when an initial climate-related change triggers
a chain of interconnected consequences across environmental, social, or economic
systems. Options a, b, and c illustrate such cascading effects. Option d is not a cascading
impact because it involves no secondary or downstream consequences.
