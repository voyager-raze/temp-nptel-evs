   1 Which of the following best defines noise pollution?
        a) Any sound louder than 60 dB
        b) Unwanted sound that produces undesired physiological or psychological effects in individuals
        c) Sound produced only by industrial activities
        d) Sound that travels faster than the speed of light
                 ANSWER

                 Noise is defined as unwanted sound that produces undesired physiological or psychological
                 effects in individuals. It can arise from traffic, industrial/construction activities, and public
                 events.

   2 Sound waves are best described as:
          a) Transverse electromagnetic waves
          b) Alternating waves of compression and rarefaction that cause local changes in air pressure
          c) Longitudinal waves that travel only through solids
          d) Continuous waves with constant amplitude
                      ANSWER

   Sound waves are alternating waves of compression and rarefaction which result in local
   changes in air density and therefore pressure. Unlike light, sound waves need a medium (fluid
   or solid) to propagate. These pressure changes are detected by the human ear.

 5 The formula for Sound Power Level (Lw) is:
          a) Lw = 20 log (W/10¹²)
          b) Lw = log (W/10¹²)
          c) Lw = 10 log (P²/P²)
          d) Lw = 10 log (W/10¹²)
          ANSWER
          Sound Power Level Lw = 10 log (W/10¹²) where the reference power level is 10¹² W.
          Similarly, Sound Intensity Level LI = 10 log(I/10¹²) W/m². The factor of 10 (not 20) is used

          a) 2 times
          b) 5 times
          c) 10 times
          d) 20 times
          ANSWER
              The decibel scale is logarithmic. 10 dB = 10 log 10¹; 20 dB = 10 log 10². The difference is 10

     7 What is the sound pressure level (SPL) for two identical sources each at 80 dB?
               a) 80 dB
               b) 83 dB
               c) 160 dB
               d) 90 dB

              ANSWER
              When two identical sources of equal SPL are combined, the total SPL = L + 10 log(N), where
              N = number of sources. Total = 80 + 10 log(2) = 80 + 10 × 0.301 = 80 + 3  83 dB. Adding
              two identical sources always increases the level by approximately 3 dB, not double the SPL.

9 The A-weighting network in a sound level meter:
     a) Approximates human response to high intensity sounds
     b) Removes low frequencies moderately
     c) Approximates human response to low intensity sounds with severe removal of low frequencies
     d) Gives equal weight to all frequencies
    ANSWER
    There are three internationally accepted weighting scales: A-network approximates human
    response to low intensity sound and removes low frequencies severely. B-network
    approximates human response to medium intensity sound with moderate low frequency
    removal. C-network approximates human response to high intensity sound with negligible
    removal of low frequencies.

    A pure tone source emits 90 dB at 100 Hz. What is the expected

    reading on the A-weighting network if the A-network correction factor

10  at 100 Hz is -19.1 dB?

    a)  109.1 dB(A)

    b)  90 dB(A)

    c)  70.9 dB(A)

    d)  84.4 dB(A)

    ANSWER
    At 100 Hz, the A-network subtracts 19.1 dB from the actual reading. Therefore: A-network
    reading = 90 - 19.1 = 70.9  71 dB(A). At 1000 Hz, the correction factor for all networks is 0,
    so the reading remains 90 dB. This illustrates how the A-filter suppresses low-frequency
    sounds.

    At 100 Hz, a source emits 90 dB. The B-network correction is -5.6 dB

    and C-network correction is -0.3 dB. What are the B and C network

11  readings respectively?

    a)  84.4 dB(B) and 89.7 dB(C)

    b)  95.6 dB(B) and 90.3 dB(C)

    c)  90 dB(B) and 90 dB(C)

    d)  70.9 dB(B) and 84.4 dB(C)

    ANSWER

    B-network reading = 90 - 5.6 = 84.4 dB(B). C-network reading = 90 - 0.3 = 89.7  90 dB(C).
    The C-network provides the least attenuation at low frequencies, so its reading is closest to the
    unweighted value. The A-network gives the most attenuation at low frequencies (-19.1 dB).
12  The normal range of human hearing is approximately:

    a)      20 Hz to 20,000 Hz

    b)      20 Hz to 200,000 Hz

    c)      200 Hz to 20,000 Hz

    d)      2 Hz to 2000 Hz

    ANSWER

    Women and children can hear from 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Young healthy males typically hear
    from 20 to 16,000 Hz. The speech range is 500 to 2000 Hz. The ear is most sensitive between
    2000 to 5000 Hz. The threshold of hearing is 20 µPa or 0 dB.

13 An octave is defined as:
         a) 8 musical notes
         b) The frequency interval between a given frequency and one that is triple that frequency
         c) The frequency interval between a given frequency and the frequency double that value
         d) A range of 1000 Hz
         ANSWER
         An octave is the frequency interval between a given frequency and the frequency double that
         value. For example, 22 Hz to 44 Hz is the first octave, followed by 44 to 88 Hz, 88 to 175 Hz,
         etc. Octave bands are used in noise analysis and control to divide noise spectra.

14  The geometric mean frequency of the octave band covering 700­1400 Hz is:

    a) 1000 Hz

    b) 1050 Hz

    c) 700 Hz

    d) 1400 Hz

    ANSWER
    The geometric mean frequency = (700 × 1400) = 980,000  990 Hz  1000 Hz. This is the
    standard octave band with geometric mean of 1000 Hz. The 1000 Hz band is the reference
    frequency for defining the phon (loudness level).

15  A 'phon' is defined as:

    a) The unit of sound power

    b) The sound pressure level of a 1000 Hz pure tone in decibels

    c) The threshold of hearing in dB

    d) The weighting factor at low frequencies

    ANSWER

    Phon is a psycho-acoustic unit equal to the sound pressure level (in dB) of a 1000 Hz pure tone.
    Two pure tones at the same sound pressure level but different frequencies have different
    loudness levels (in phons) because the ear's sensitivity varies with frequency. The Fletcher-
    Munson equal-loudness contours represent this variation.

16 Temporary Threshold Shift (TTS) in hearing:
       a) Is permanent and cannot be recovered
       b) Occurs only at sound levels below 40 dB
       c) Only affects the C-weighting network response
       d) Involves ringing, discomfort or muffling; occurs in first 2 h of exposure with recovery in 16­24 h

    ANSWER

    Temporary Threshold Shift (TTS) involves symptoms like ringing in the ear, discomfort or
    muffling of sound. It occurs within the first 2 hours of exposure and recovery takes about 16 to
    24 hours after exposure ends. Permanent Threshold Shift (PTS) is the irreversible form,
    characterised by hearing loss between 3000­6000 Hz with a dip (notch) at 4000 Hz.
17  Hearing loss due to aging is termed:

    a)      Presbycusis

    b)      Tinnitus

    c)      Temporary Threshold Shift

    d)      High-frequency notch

    ANSWER

    Age-related degeneration of hearing responses is called presbycusis. It must be distinguished
    from noise-induced hearing loss. Other causes of hearing loss include drugs, disease, and
    physical head injury. Noise-induced hearing loss shows a characteristic high-frequency notch
    (dip at 4000 Hz) that broadens in both directions over time.

18  The NIOSH occupational noise exposure limit for an 8-hour workday is:

    a)      70 dB(A)

    b)      75 dB(A)

    c)      85 dB(A)

    d)      100 dB(A)

    ANSWER
    Regular and prolonged exposures to noise at or above 85 dB(A) averaged over 8 hours per day
    are considered hazardous by NIOSH. At 100 dB(A), unprotected exposure of more than 15
    minutes per day risks permanent hearing loss. At 110 dB(A), even 1.5 minutes of unprotected
    exposure per day risks permanent hearing loss.
19  According to the Inverse Square Law for outdoor sound transmission,
    if the distance from a point source doubles, the Sound Pressure Level (SPL):

    a)       Increases by 6 dB

    b)       Decreases by 6 dB

    c)       Decreases by 3 dB

    d)       Remains the same

    ANSWER
    The Inverse Square Law states that sound intensity is inversely proportional to the square of
    distance (I  1/r²). Since SPL = 10 log(I/I), when distance doubles: L = 10 log(r²/r²) = 20
    log(r/r) = 20 log(1/2) = -20 × 0.301 = -6 dB. So every doubling of distance reduces SPL by
    6 dB.

    Under India's Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000,

    the noise level at the boundary of a public place using a loudspeaker shall not

20  exceed:

    a)       5 dB(A) above ambient standards or 75 dB(A), whichever is lower

    b)       10 dB(A) above ambient standards or 75 dB(A), whichever is lower

    c)       10 dB(A) above ambient standards or 85 dB(A), whichever is lower

    d)       15 dB(A) above ambient standards regardless of zone

    ANSWER
    Under India's Noise Pollution Rules 2000: the noise level at the boundary of a public place
    using loudspeakers/PA systems shall not exceed 10 dB(A) above the ambient noise standards
    for the area OR 75 dB(A), whichever is lower. For privately owned sound systems at the
    boundary of a private place, the limit is 5 dB(A) above ambient noise standards. A 100-metre
    area around hospitals, educational institutions and courts may be declared a silence zone.
