WEEK 12: Environmental policies and legislation in India
Q1.
The Oleum Gas Leak case (1986) is significant primarily because it transformed an existing
legal doctrine into a stricter form?
a) The Precautionary Principle into the Polluter Pays Principle
b) The Doctrine of Strict Liability into the Doctrine of Absolute Liability
c) The Public Trust Doctrine into Sustainable Development
d) Criminal Procedure Code liability into Civil liability
Correct Answer: Option (b)
Solution: Before this case, courts applied 'strict liability' which allowed exceptions. The
Supreme Court modified it to 'absolute liability' meaning no exceptions apply when hazardous
industries cause harm near human habitation.
Q2.
In the Ratlam Municipality case (1980), the municipality argued it lacked funds to provide
sanitation. What did the Supreme Court decide regarding this defence?
a) It was accepted since financial constraints are a valid legal excuse
b) The case was transferred to the High Court for reassessment
c) The municipality was ordered to act within six months
d) Residents were asked to fund the facilities themselves
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: The Supreme Court ruled that a municipality cannot escape its statutory duty to
provide basic sanitation by pleading financial incapacity.
Q3.
The Taj Trapezium Zone (TTZ) case involved protecting 255 historic monuments. Which
combination of remedies did the Supreme Court order?
a) Shut down the Mathura refinery permanently and plant trees
b) Ban coal use in the zone, switch industries to CNG, and relocate tanneries
c) Impose fines on industries and require them to install filters
d) Create a 10 km no-industry buffer around the Taj Mahal only
Correct Answer: Option (b)
Solution: The Court ordered a ban on coal, directed industries to shift to Compressed Natural
Gas (CNG), asked tanneries to relocate out of the TTZ, and required a green belt around the
Mathura refinery.
Q4.
In the Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum case (1996), tanneries argued they were major foreign
exchange earners and employers. How did the Supreme Court balance this against
environmental harm?
a) It allowed tanneries to continue operations as economic benefits outweighed harm
b) It transferred enforcement entirely to the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board
c) It shut down all tanneries permanently and compensated workers from government funds
d) It applied the Polluter Pays Principle, fined each tannery 10,000, and required them to fund
a common effluent treatment plant
Correct Answer: Option (d)
Solution: The Court acknowledged both sides but upheld the Polluter Pays Principle and
Sustainable Development doctrine holding that economic importance could not justify
environmental destruction. Each tannery paid a fine and contributed to a Common Effluent
Treatment Plant (CETP).
Q5.
In the Alaknanda Hydropower Project case (NGT, 2016), the company argued the 2013
Uttarakhand floods were an 'Act of God' and it bore no liability. How did the NGT respond?
a) The NGT accepted the Act of God defence and dismissed the petition
b) The NGT ruled the company bore 100% liability since the dam amplified flooding
c) The NGT found that construction muck (approx. 30% of damage) made the company
partially liable despite the natural disaster
d) The NGT ordered a fresh environmental clearance before deciding on liability
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: The NGT applied the No-Fault Liability principle and found that phyllite and
construction debris from the project contributed about 30% of the damage, meaning the Act of
God defence did not fully shield the company. Each applicant received Rs. 1 lakh
compensation.
Q6.
A rapid EIA of the River Ib Barrage evaluated barrage heights of 4 m, 5 m, and 6 m. Even the
tallest option (6 m) was found inadequate. What was the main reason?
a) The 6 m barrage would submerge too many coal mines, violating environmental law
b) The total annual water demand was 94.8 MCM/year, but even the 6 m option could only
satisfy about 14.6% of that
c) The Digital Elevation Model showed the site was geologically unstable
d) Evaporation losses exceeded inflow during monsoon months
Correct Answer: Option (b)
Solution: The estimated water flow into the river was only 65.5 MCM/year, already less than
demand. The 6 m barrage met just 14.6% of the 94.8 MCM/year requirement, indicating that a
barrage based solution was unlikely to meet the projected demand. Refer Lecture 5.
Q7.
The Bishnoi movement of 1730 is regarded as one of India's earliest tree protection protests.
What was the immediate cause of the conflict in the village of Khejarli?
a) Villagers protested against a new road cutting through their forest
b) King Abhay Singh ordered his men to clear Khejri trees for wood to build his palace
c) A British company obtained a logging permit for the Thar desert region
d) The state government planned to replace natural trees with teak plantations
Correct Answer: Option (b)
Solution: The Bishnoi community opposed the felling of Khejri trees ordered by King Abhay
Singh's officials. Their sacrifice at Khejarli became one of the earliest examples of organized
environmental conservation in India.
Q8.
Which of the following statements regarding the Paris Agreement and India's Nationally
Determined Contributions (NDC) is incorrect?
a) India committed to reducing emissions intensity by 33­35% below 2005 levels.
b) India pledged to achieve 40% installed electricity capacity from non fossil fuel sources by

countries determine their own mitigation targets.

Q10.
Under the Biomedical Waste Management Rules (1998), which colour coded container is used
for sharp metallic waste such as needles and scalpels, and which is used for broken glassware
and medicine vials?
a) Yellow for sharps; red for glassware
b) Red for sharps; white for glassware
c) Blue for sharps; white for glassware
d) White for sharps; blue for glassware
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: Sharp metallic wastes are collected in blue containers, while broken glassware and
medicine vials are placed in white containers to ensure safe segregation and disposal.
Q11.
Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) was a key innovation in Indian waste rules. Which of
the following rules first clearly defined EPR?
a) Fly Ash Rules 1999
b) Hazardous Waste Rules 1989
c) E Waste Rules 2011 (clarified in 2022)
d) Municipal Solid Waste Rules 2000
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: EPR places responsibility on producers for managing products after their useful life.
It aims to improve recycling, recovery and environmentally sound disposal of electronic waste.
Q12.
The Doon Valley case (1987) is celebrated as the first Supreme Court judgment to apply a key
environmental doctrine in India. Which doctrine was applied, and what was the court's ruling?
a) Polluter Pays Principle; all mining companies were fined and ordered to pay for reforestation
b) Doctrine of Absolute Liability; mine owners were ordered to pay compensation to all
residents of Dehradun
c) Public Trust Doctrine; the valley was declared state property and transferred to a government
conservation authority
d) Doctrine of Sustainable Development; all mines except three were ordered closed, and
economic development was held not to justify environmental degradation
Correct Answer: Option (d)
Solution: The Supreme Court ruled that economic development should not occur at the cost of
environmental destruction. Most limestone mines in the Doon Valley were ordered to shut
down.
Q13.
In M.C. Mehta vs. Kamal Nath (1996), the Supreme Court held that the Himachal Pradesh
government had violated an important doctrine by leasing protected forest land on the banks
of the Beas River to a motel. Which doctrine was this?
a) Doctrine of Absolute Liability
b) Precautionary Principle
c) Public Trust Doctrine
d) Doctrine of Sustainable Development
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: The Court held that natural resources are held by the State in trust for public use and
enjoyment. Such resources cannot be transferred for private commercial exploitation.
Q14.
Which of the following was a major reason for rejecting the proposed vegetable oil industry
near Himayat Sagar and Osman Sagar reservoirs?
a) The project was located inside a wildlife sanctuary
b) The project violated coastal regulation norms
c) It posed a potential threat to major drinking water sources
d) The project lacked foreign investment approval
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: The proposed vegetable oil industry was rejected because it was located near the
Himayat Sagar and Osman Sagar reservoirs, which were important sources of drinking water
for Hyderabad. The Supreme Court held that the project posed a potential threat to major
drinking water reservoirs serving Hyderabad.
Q15.
The Supreme Court's order requiring cinema halls and television networks to promote
environmental awareness originated from:
a) M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Environmental Education Case)
b) Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum Case
c) Ratlam Municipality Case
d) Almitra Patel Case
Correct Answer: Option (a)
Solution: In M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (Environmental Education Case), the Supreme Court
emphasized the importance of environmental awareness among citizens. The Court directed
cinema halls to display environmental messages and required television and radio networks to
broadcast programmes on environmental protection, recognizing environmental education as
essential for sustainable development.
Q16.
Which of the following principles places the burden of proof on the project proponent to
demonstrate environmental safety?
a) Polluter Pays Principle
b) Public Trust Doctrine
c) Precautionary Principle
d) Absolute Liability
Correct Answer: Option (c)
Solution: Under the Precautionary Principle, when there is a potential risk of serious
environmental harm, the project proponent must prove that the activity is environmentally safe.
The burden of proof lies on the proposer, not on those raising environmental concerns.
Q17.
The creation of CAMPA (Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning
Authority) emerged from judicial intervention related to:
a) Taj Trapezium conservation
b) Godavarman forest case
c) Oleum gas leakage
d) Ratlam sanitation dispute
Correct Answer: Option (b)
Solution: CAMPA was established following directions issued in the Godavarman forest case
to monitor compensatory afforestation, oversee funds collected for forest diversion, and
support the preservation of forests.

Q19.
A State Government argues that Solid Waste Management Rules cannot be implemented
immediately due to inadequate infrastructure and therefore seeks a phased implementation
