QUESTION 1:

Which of the following activities is not an example of reuse?

a. Refilling a beverage bottle for repeated use
b. Using a refillable shopping bag multiple times
c. Melting and reprocessing plastic bottles to manufacture new bottles
d. Donating used clothing for continued use by others

Correct answer: c. Melting and reprocessing plastic bottles to manufacture new
bottles

Solution: Refer to Slide #14 of Lecture #2. Reuse involves using a product again with little
or no modification, whereas recycling involves processing waste materials to create new
products. Option (c) is an example of recycling, not reuse.

QUESTION 2:

According to the circular economy hierarchy, which of the following sequences correctly
arranges the listed "R" strategies in increasing order of circularity (from least circular to
most circular)?

      a. Recycle < Repurpose < Rethink < Repair
      b. Repurpose < Recycle < Repair < Rethink
      c. Recycle < Repurpose < Repair < Rethink
      d. Recycle < Repair < Repurpose < Rethink

Correct answer: c. Recycle < Repurpose < Repair < Rethink

Solution: Refer to Slide #8 of Lecture #2. Recycling recovers materials after a product
reaches the end of its useful life and is generally less circular than strategies that retain
product value. Repurposing gives a product a new function, while repairing extends its
original use with minimal resource input. Rethinking involves redesigning products,
services, or consumption patterns to avoid waste generation in the first place and is
therefore considered the most circular strategy among the options.

    b. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

QUESTION 4:

A company decides to remanufacture electric motors instead of producing new ones. A
life-cycle assessment shows that manufacturing a new motor results in 5.0 tonnes of CO2
emissions, whereas remanufacturing an existing motor results in 1.5 tonnes of CO2
emissions. If the company remanufactures 65 motors, how much CO2 emissions are
avoided compared with manufacturing the same number of new motors?

    a. 201.5 tonnes CO2
    b. 227.5 tonnes CO2
    c. 240.5 tonnes CO2
    d. 280.4 tonnes CO2

Correct answer: b. 227.5 tonnes CO2
Solution:
Emission savings per motor: 5.0 ­ 1.5 = 3.5 tonnes CO2
For 65 motors: 65 × 3.5 = 227.5 tonnes CO2
Remanufacturing each motor avoids 3.5 tonnes of CO2 emissions compared with
manufacturing a new motor. Therefore, remanufacturing 65 motors results in a total
emissions savings of 227.5 tonnes of CO2.
QUESTION 5:

Which of the following best illustrates a "rethink" strategy in a circular economy, where a
product or system is fundamentally redesigned to deliver greater value and resource
efficiency?

      a. Designing beverage containers that can be returned, cleaned, and refilled multiple
          times.

      b. Recovering phosphorus from municipal wastewater for fertilizer production.
      c. Replacing individual office printers with a centralized on-demand printing service.
      d. Repairing industrial motors to extend their operational lifespan.

Correct answer: c. Replacing individual office printers with a centralized on-demand
printing service.

Solution: Refer to Slide #10 of Lecture #2. A rethink strategy involves fundamentally
changing how a product or service is delivered to reduce resource consumption and
improve utilization. Replacing individual printers with a shared on-demand printing service
rethinks the conventional ownership model. In contrast, options (a), (b), and (d) represent
reuse, resource recovery, and repair, respectively.

QUESTION 6:

Within the framework of a circular economy, what is the most significant advantage of
repurposing or recycling decommissioned wind turbine blades?

    a. It reduces the need for landfill disposal while retaining the value embedded in
         composite materials.

    b. It decreases the environmental impacts associated with the extraction and
         processing of virgin construction materials.

    c. It creates opportunities for secondary applications such as bridges, noise barriers,
         and architectural structures.

    d. It extends the usefulness of blade materials beyond their original function, thereby
         slowing resource throughput in the economy.

Correct answer: d. It extends the usefulness of blade materials beyond their original
function, thereby slowing resource throughput in the economy.
Solution: All four statements are valid benefits. However, from a circular economy
perspective, the most fundamental objective is to retain the value of materials for as long
as possible and reduce the flow of virgin resources through the economy. Repurposing
wind turbine blades directly achieves this by extending the useful life of embedded
materials.
QUESTION 7:

Two cities consume the same quantity of energy and materials each year. City A recovers
and reuses a substantial fraction of its waste streams, whereas City B primarily relies on
landfill disposal. Which statement best describes the likely difference in their urban
metabolisms?

    a. City A is likely to have a lower material throughput per unit of useful service
         delivered.

    b. City A is likely to exhibit higher material circulation within the system and lower
         dependence on virgin resource inputs.

    c. City A is likely to accumulate waste stocks more rapidly because recovered
         materials remain within the city.

    d. City A is likely to have a larger metabolic footprint because resource recovery
         processes require additional energy and infrastructure.

Correct answer: b. City A is likely to exhibit higher material circulation within the
system and lower dependence on virgin resource inputs.

Solution: By recovering and reusing a larger fraction of its waste streams, City A keeps
materials in productive use for longer periods, increasing material circulation within the
urban system. This reduces the need for virgin resource extraction and reflects a more
circular and resource-efficient urban metabolism compared with City B, which primarily
relies on landfill disposal.

QUESTION 9:

Two companies adopt different approaches for managing end-of-life office chairs. One
company repairs damaged chairs and returns them to service, while the other recycles
the chairs by processing them into raw materials for new products. According to the
Butterfly Diagram of the circular economy, why is the first approach generally considered
more circular?

      a. Repair requires less labor and economic activity than recycling.
      b. Repair preserves a greater proportion of the product's embodied value than

          material recycling.
     c. Repair completely eliminates the need for future resource extraction.
      d. Repair prevents the generation of waste throughout the product life cycle.

Correct answer: b. Repair preserves a greater proportion of the product's embodied
value than material recycling.
Solution: The Butterfly Diagram prioritizes strategies that retain the highest value of
products, components, and materials. By keeping the chair in use with minimal
processing, repair preserves more embodied value than recycling, which breaks the
product down to its constituent materials.

QUESTION 10:

A city achieves a recycling rate of 95% for aluminum cans. Which statement best reflects
a key limitation of relying solely on circular economy strategies to achieve long-term
resource sustainability?

      a. High recycling rates can substantially reduce demand for primary aluminum, but
          may not eliminate it because material losses occur and overall consumption can
          continue to grow.

      b. Once recycling rates exceed a critical threshold, the aluminum cycle becomes
          effectively self-sustaining and independent of virgin resource inputs.

      c. High recycling rates ensure that the environmental impacts associated with
          aluminum production are largely eliminated over time.

      d. Increasing recycling efficiency is sufficient to decouple economic growth from
          resource extraction under all circumstances.

Correct answer: a. High recycling rates can substantially reduce demand for primary
aluminum, but may not eliminate it because material losses occur and overall
consumption can continue to grow.
Solution: Even highly efficient recycling systems experience material losses, and
increasing consumption can outpace gains from resource recovery. Therefore, high
recycling rates alone do not guarantee complete circularity or eliminate the need for virgin
resource extraction.
