Question 1:
Consider the following statements regarding water resources and water use:

         I. Groundwater constitutes a larger share of freshwater resources than all fresh surface water
         combined.
         II. Since groundwater forms nearly one-third of freshwater resources, it is generally more accessible
         than surface water.
         III. The amount of usable freshwater on Earth is less than 0.1% of the planet's total water resources.
         IV. Most freshwater surface water is stored in rivers.
Which of the above statements are correct?
a. I and II only
b. I and III only
c. II and IV only
d. I, III and IV only
Answer: b. I and III only
Detailed Solution:
I. Correct. Groundwater constitutes about 30.1% of freshwater resources, whereas surface water accounts for
only about 0.3%.
II. Incorrect. A large fraction of groundwater is located at great depths and is therefore not easily accessible.
III. Correct. Only a very small fraction of the Earth's total water resources is available as usable freshwater,
amounting to less than 0.1% of the total water on Earth.
IV. Incorrect. Most freshwater surface water is stored in lakes (87%), while rivers contain only about 2%.
Hence, only Statements I and III are correct.
Question 2:
Consider the following statements regarding water withdrawal and consumption:

         I. Water used for drinking and cooking is generally considered a return flow.
         II. Municipal wastewater discharge can exceed municipal water supply.
         III. Water used for bathing generally contributes to wastewater generation.
         IV. Domestic water use in urban areas is predominantly non-consumptive in nature.
Which of the above statements are correct?
a. I and II only
b. I, II and III only
c. II and III only
d. II, III and IV only
Answer: d. II, III and IV only
Detailed Solution:
I. Incorrect. Water used for drinking and cooking is a consumptive use and is generally not returned directly
to the water source.
II. Correct. Municipal wastewater discharge may exceed municipal water supply due to contributions from
private tube wells and tanker water.
III. Correct. Bathing water enters the sewer system and contributes to wastewater generation.
IV. Correct. In urban areas, most domestic water is used for bathing, washing, cleaning, and similar
activities that generate wastewater. Therefore, urban domestic water use is predominantly non-consumptive
in nature, with a large fraction returning as wastewater.
Hence, Statements II, III and IV are correct.
Question 3:

Consider the following pollution sources:

         I. Industrial effluent discharged through a pipeline.

         II. Stormwater runoff from urban roads.

         III. Agricultural runoff carrying fertilizers and pesticides.

         IV. Wastewater treatment plant discharge.

         V. Leachate entering a river through a distinct and identifiable channel.

How many of the above are point sources of pollution?

a. 2
b. 3

c. 4

d. 5

Answer: b. 3

Detailed Solution:

Point sources are pollution sources that originate from a specific, identifiable location such as a pipe, drain,
or outlet.
Source I is a point source because industrial effluent is discharged through a clearly identifiable pipeline.
Source II is a non-point source. Stormwater runoff originates over large urban surfaces such as roads,
parking lots, and pavements rather than from a single discharge location.
Source III is a non-point source because rainwater washes over huge farming fields and enters rivers or lakes
from many different places at once, rather than from a single pipe.
Source IV is a point source because wastewater treatment plants discharge treated effluent through
designated outlets.
Source V is a point source because the leachate enters the water body through a specific and identifiable
channel.
Thus, Sources I, IV, and V are point sources, giving a total of three point sources.

Question 12:
Assertion (A): A wastewater sample having a higher ThOD than another sample may exhibit a lower BOD.
Reason (R): ThOD represents the total oxygen required for complete chemical oxidation, whereas BOD
depends on the biodegradable fraction oxidized within five days.
Choose the correct option:
a. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
b. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
c. A is true, but R is false.
d. A is false, but R is true.
Answer: a. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Detailed Solution:
ThOD represents the theoretical oxygen required for complete oxidation of a compound.

BOD represents the oxygen consumed due to biological oxidation during the first five days.

A sample may have a high ThOD but contain compounds that are not readily biodegradable. In such a case,
its BOD may be lower than that of another sample having a smaller ThOD but a larger biodegradable
fraction.

Therefore, the Assertion is true. The Reason is also true and correctly explains the Assertion.

Question 16:
Consider the following statements regarding the dissolved oxygen sag curve:

         I. Prior to the critical point, the rate of reaeration exceeds the rate of deoxygenation.
         II. At the critical point, the dissolved oxygen concentration is zero.
         III. Beyond the critical point, the river begins to recover because the rate of reaeration exceeds the
         rate of deoxygenation.
         IV. The self-purification capacity of a river is represented by the DO sag curve.
Which of the above statements are correct?
a. I and II only
b. II and III only
c. III and IV only
d. II, III and IV only
Answer: c. III and IV only
Detailed Solution:
Statement I: Incorrect.
Prior to the critical point, the rate of deoxygenation exceeds the rate of reaeration, causing the dissolved
oxygen deficit to increase.

Statement II: Incorrect.
At the critical point, the dissolved oxygen concentration is minimum, but it is not necessarily zero. Zero DO
is possible only under extremely severe pollution conditions.

Statement III: Correct.
Beyond the critical point, the rate of reaeration exceeds the rate of deoxygenation, causing the river to
recover and the dissolved oxygen concentration to increase.

Statement IV: Correct.
The dissolved oxygen sag curve represents the self-purification capacity of the river, showing the combined
effect of deoxygenation and reaeration.
Therefore, statements III and IV are correct.
Question 17:
Municipal wastewater discharged into a river contains organic nitrogen, which undergoes a series of
transformations under aerobic conditions. Which one of the following represents the correct sequence of the
predominant nitrogen species with increasing distance downstream from the point of discharge?
a. Organic nitrogen  Ammonia nitrogen  Nitrite nitrogen  Nitrate nitrogen
b. Organic nitrogen  Nitrite nitrogen  Nitrate nitrogen  Ammonia nitrogen
c. Organic nitrogen  Ammonia nitrogen  Nitrate nitrogen  Nitrite nitrogen
d. Organic nitrogen  Nitrate nitrogen  Nitrite nitrogen  Ammonia nitrogen
Answer: a. Organic nitrogen  Ammonia nitrogen  Nitrite nitrogen  Nitrate nitrogen
Detailed Solution:
Organic nitrogen present in wastewater is first converted to ammonia nitrogen by biological decomposition.
Under aerobic conditions, Nitrosomonas bacteria oxidize ammonia nitrogen to nitrite nitrogen, which is
subsequently oxidized to nitrate nitrogen by Nitrobacter bacteria. Nitrate is the most stable oxidized form of
nitrogen.

Therefore, the sequence of predominant nitrogen species with increasing distance downstream is:
Organic nitrogen  Ammonia nitrogen  Nitrite nitrogen  Nitrate nitrogen

Question 20:
Consider the following statements regarding wastewater treatment:

         I. Primary treatment removes dissolved biodegradable organic matter.
         II. Secondary treatment employs biological processes.
         III. Conventional wastewater treatment consists of primary and secondary treatment.
         IV. Disinfection is generally carried out before secondary treatment.
Which of the above statements are correct?
a. I and II only
b. I, III and IV only
c. II and III only
d. II, III and IV only
Answer: c. II and III only
Detailed Solution:
Statement I: Incorrect
Primary treatment removes objectionable solids present in wastewater by physical processes such as
screening and grit removal. Dissolved biodegradable organic matter, represented by BOD, is removed
during secondary treatment.

Statement II: Correct
Secondary treatment aims at the removal of biodegradable organic matter. Biological processes such as
activated sludge process and trickling filters are commonly employed for this purpose.

Statement III: Correct
Primary treatment followed by secondary treatment constitutes conventional wastewater treatment. After
these stages, disinfection is generally carried out prior to discharge.

Statement IV: Incorrect
Secondary treatment promotes microbial activity, resulting in a high concentration of microorganisms in the
treated effluent. Therefore, disinfection is carried out after secondary treatment to reduce the possibility of
pathogenic microorganisms being present in the discharged water.

Hence, Statements II and III are correct.
