QUESTION 1:

A country decides to rapidly phase out fossil fuels without investing adequately in
alternative energy infrastructure. Which dimension of the Global Energy Trilemma is most
likely to be adversely affected in the short term?

I. Energy security
II. Energy equity
III. Environmental sustainability

    a. I only
    b. I and II only
    c. II and III only
    d. I, II, and III

Solution: Refer to Slides #5 of Lecture #1. The most immediate and direct consequence

I. It is a passive solar heating system.
II. It stores solar energy in a high thermal-mass wall.
III. It requires photovoltaic panels to generate electricity.
IV. It can help reduce space-heating demand in buildings.

     a. I and II only
     b. I, II, and IV only

Solution: Refer to Slides #18­#19 of Lecture #4. A Trombe wall is a passive solar design

QUESTION 3:

Assertion (A): Bifacial photovoltaic (PV) modules can generate more electricity than
monofacial PV modules under suitable operating conditions.

Reason (R): Bifacial PV modules are capable of capturing solar radiation from both their
front and rear surfaces.

     a. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
      b. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
      c. A is true, but R is false.
      d. A is false, but R is true.

Correct answer: a. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
Solution: Refer to Slides #11 & #12 of Lecture #3. The assertion is true because bifacial
PV modules can produce more electricity than conventional monofacial modules when
installed under favorable conditions. The reason is also true, as bifacial modules are
designed to capture solar radiation on both sides of the panel. In addition to direct sunlight
incident on the front surface, the rear surface can utilize reflected and diffuse radiation
from the ground and surrounding surfaces (albedo radiation). This additional light capture
increases the total solar energy received by the module, leading to higher electricity
generation compared to monofacial modules, which can harvest light only from the front
surface.

    c. To convert solar energy directly into electricity without a turbine
    d. To eliminate heat losses from the solar receiver

QUESTION 6:

According to Betz's law, a wind turbine cannot extract all of the kinetic energy from the
wind because

    a. the air must retain some kinetic energy after passing through the rotor.
    b. the wind speed must remain unchanged across the rotor plane.
    c. the air accelerates as it passes through the rotor, reducing energy extraction.
    d. the kinetic energy of the wind is independent of its velocity.

Correct answer: a. the air must retain some kinetic energy after passing through the
rotor.
Solution: Refer to Slide #14 of Lecture #5. A wind turbine generates power by extracting
a portion of the wind's kinetic energy and reducing its velocity. However, the air must
retain some kinetic energy after passing through the rotor so that it can continue flowing
downstream. If all of the kinetic energy were extracted, the air would come to a complete
stop behind the turbine, preventing any further airflow through the rotor and making
continuous power generation impossible. This physical constraint establishes the
theoretical upper limit on wind energy extraction described by Betz's law.

onshore wind farms?

    a. They eliminate the need for transmission infrastructure.
    b. They generally experience stronger and more consistent wind resources.
    c. They require smaller turbines to achieve the same power output.
    d. They are less expensive to install and maintain.

Correct answer: b. They generally experience stronger and more consistent wind
resources.
Solution: Refer to Slide #18 of Lecture #5. Offshore locations typically experience higher

increase energy production and improve the capacity factor of offshore wind turbines,
making offshore wind farms an attractive option despite their higher installation and
maintenance costs.

QUESTION 8:

A passive solar building receives 5,000 kWh of useful solar heat through its south-facing
windows during the winter. If the building experiences a total heat loss of 4,000 kWh over
the same period, what is the additional heating energy required from a backup heating
system?

      a. 0 kWh
      b. 1,000 kWh
     c. 4,000 kWh
     d. 5,000 kWh

Correct answer: a. 0 kWh
Solution: The solar heat gain (5,000 kWh) exceeds the building's heat loss (4,000 kWh).
Since the available solar heat is sufficient to offset all heat losses, no additional heating
is required from a backup system. The excess 1,000 kWh represents a net heat surplus.

QUESTION 9:

For a fixed solar photovoltaic (PV) system in the Northern Hemisphere, increasing the
panel tilt angle beyond the annual optimum generally helps to

      a. increase winter energy capture when the sun is lower in the sky
      b. increase summer energy capture when the sun is nearly overhead
      c. eliminate reflection losses from the panel surface
      d. maintain the same solar incidence angle throughout the year
Correct answer: a. increase winter energy capture when the sun is lower in the sky
Solution: Refer to Slide #8 of Lecture #3. In winter, the sun follows a lower path across
the sky. A steeper panel tilt improves the angle at which sunlight strikes the module,
thereby increasing winter solar energy capture.

QUESTION 10:

Which feature most clearly distinguishes an active solar heating system from a passive
solar heating system?

      a. Collection of solar energy through south-facing glazing
      b. Storage of heat in materials with high thermal mass
      c. Distribution of heat by natural convection and radiation
      d. Heat transfer assisted by pumps or fans

Correct answer: d. Heat transfer assisted by pumps or fans
Solution: Active solar heating systems use mechanical devices such as pumps or fans to
circulate a heat-transfer fluid and distribute solar heat. Passive solar heating systems rely
on building design, thermal mass, and natural heat transfer mechanisms such as
conduction, convection, and radiation.

QUESTION 13:

Which of the following pathways can be used to produce green hydrogen directly using
solar energy?

      a. Photoelectrochemical water splitting
      b. Photocatalytic water splitting
      c. Photovoltaic-powered water electrolysis
      d. Solar-powered alkaline electrolysis

Correct answer(s):

      a. Photoelectrochemical water splitting
      b. Photocatalytic water splitting
Solution: Refer to Slide #25 of Lecture #4. Photoelectrochemical and photocatalytic
systems use sunlight directly to drive water-splitting reactions. In contrast, photovoltaic-
powered and solar-powered electrolysis first convert solar energy into electricity, which is
then used to produce hydrogen.
