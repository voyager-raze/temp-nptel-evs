QUESTION 1:

Which of the following is an example of a secondary air pollutant, formed in the
atmosphere through chemical reactions involving primary pollutants?

a. Volatile organic compounds
b. Sulphur dioxide
c. Ground-level ozone
d. Carbon monoxide

Correct answer: c. Ground-level ozone

Solution: Refer to Slide #14 of Lecture #1. Ground-level ozone (O3) is a secondary air
pollutant because it is formed in the atmosphere through reactions between nitrogen
oxides (NO) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in the presence of sunlight. The
other options are primary pollutants emitted directly from sources.

QUESTION 2:

Which of the following atmospheric conditions is most conducive to the formation of a
radiation inversion?

    a. Cloudy conditions that limit nighttime heat loss
    b. Intense daytime heating of the Earth's surface
    c. Rainfall associated with monsoon weather systems
    d. Clear, calm nights that promote rapid surface cooling.

Correct answer: d. Clear, calm nights that promote rapid surface cooling.

Solution: Refer to Slide #15 of Lecture #2. Radiation inversions commonly occur on clear,
calm nights when the ground loses heat rapidly through radiation. This cools the air near
the surface more than the air above, creating a temperature inversion in which
temperature increases with height.

QUESTION 4:

Which characteristic of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) enables it to be the dominant
component of transboundary haze?

      a. They are largely insoluble in water.
      b. They can remain suspended in the atmosphere for extended periods and be

          transported over long distances.
      c. They have a short atmospheric residence time.
      d. They are transported only under conditions of strong winds.

Correct answer: b. They can remain suspended in the atmosphere for extended
periods and be transported over long distances.
Solution: Refer to Slide #9 of Lecture #4. Fine particles (PM2.5) have very low settling
velocities, allowing them to remain airborne for days to weeks. As a result, they can be
carried over hundreds or even thousands of kilometers by atmospheric circulation,
making them a major contributor to transboundary haze.

QUESTION 5:

In a dry atmosphere, under which condition is the atmosphere considered unstable?

    a. The dry adiabatic lapse rate is greater than the environmental lapse rate.
    b. The dry adiabatic lapse rate is equal to the environmental lapse rate.
    c. The dry adiabatic lapse rate is less than the environmental lapse rate.
    d. The moist adiabatic lapse rate is equal to the environmental lapse rate.

Correct answer: c. The dry adiabatic lapse rate is less than the environmental lapse
rate.
Solution: Refer to Slide #13 of Lecture #2. An atmosphere is unstable when the
environmental lapse rate (ELR) exceeds the dry adiabatic lapse rate (DALR). In this
case, a rising air parcel remains warmer and less dense than its surroundings, causing it
to continue rising. Therefore, atmospheric instability occurs when the DALR is less than
the ELR.
QUESTION 6:

Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) technology is an air pollution control technology
primarily used to reduce emissions of ______, converting them into harmless ______ and
______.

    a. SOx, S2, and H2
    b. NOx , N2, and H2O
    c. CO2 , C, and H2
    d. VOCs, N2, and H2O

Correct answer: b. NOx, N2 and H2O

Solution: Refer to Slide #18 of Lecture #3. Selective catalytic reduction (SCR) is an
advanced technology used to control nitrogen oxide (NO) emissions from diesel
engines. In this process, a diesel exhaust fluid (DEF), typically a urea-based solution, is
injected into the exhaust stream upstream of a catalyst. The heat of the exhaust converts
the DEF into ammonia (NH3), which then reacts with nitric oxide (NO) and nitrogen
dioxide (NO2) on the surface of the catalyst. These reactions convert NO into nitrogen
gas (N2) and water vapor (H2O), both of which are harmless and released through the
vehicle's tailpipe.

QUESTION 7:

A region is exposed to severe acid deposition over an extended period. Which of the
following environmental impacts is most likely to occur first?

    a. Complete disappearance of forest vegetation.
    b. Increase in atmospheric ozone concentration.
    c. Depletion of soil buffering capacity due to the loss of Ca2 and Mg2 ion.
    d. Enhanced carbon sequestration in terrestrial ecosystems.

Correct answer: c. Depletion of soil buffering capacity due to the loss of Ca2 and
Mg2 ion.

Solution: Refer to Slide #15 of Lecture #4. Acid deposition leaches essential base cations
such as calcium (Ca2) and magnesium (Mg2) from soils, reducing their ability to
neutralize acidity. This loss of buffering capacity is typically one of the earliest
environmental effects and can eventually lead to soil degradation, forest decline, and
aquatic ecosystem damage.
QUESTION 8:

Which of the following compounds is not considered an ozone-depleting substance
(ODS)?

      a. Methyl fluoride
      b. Methyl chloride
      c. Methyl bromide
      d. Methyl chloroform

Correct answer: a. Methyl fluoride
Solution: Refer to Slide #11 of Lecture #5. Ozone depletion is primarily caused by
compounds containing chlorine or bromine, which release reactive halogen radicals in
the stratosphere. Methyl fluoride (CH3F) contains fluorine but no chlorine or bromine and
has negligible ozone-depleting potential. In contrast, methyl chloride, methyl bromide,
and methyl chloroform are recognized ozone-depleting substances.

QUESTION 9:

A dry air parcel with an initial temperature of 30°C rises 1 km in the atmosphere. Assuming
a dry adiabatic lapse rate (DALR) of 9.8°C km­1 and an environmental temperature of
22°C at 1 km altitude, what will most likely happen to the parcel?

    a. It will continue rising.
    b. It will become neutrally buoyant.
    c. It will sink back toward the surface.
    d. It will form a temperature inversion.

Correct answer: c. It will sink back toward the surface.
Solution: Refer to Slide #13 of Lecture #2. The parcel cools at the DALR as it rises:

                                          Tparcel = 30-9.8 = 20.2°C
At 1 km, the surrounding air temperature is 22°C, which is warmer than the parcel
(20.2°C). Since the parcel is cooler and therefore denser than its surroundings, it
experiences negative buoyancy and tends to sink back toward the surface. Thus, the
atmosphere is stable under these conditions.

QUESTION 10:

The sub-index values of NO, SO2, and PM10 are 60, 90, and 40, respectively. According
to the National Air Quality Index (NAQI) released by the Government of India in the year
2015, the overall NAQI is

    a. 80
    b. 200
    c. 67
    d. 70
Correct answer: a. 90
Solution: Refer to Slide #22 of Lecture #2. According to the NAQI framework, the overall
AQI is determined by the highest sub-index among all monitored pollutants, as the
pollutant with the greatest health risk dictates the overall air quality category. Since the
sub-index values are NO = 60, SO2 = 90, and PM10 = 40, the highest sub-index is 90.
Therefore, the overall NAQI = 90.

Question 11:

Which of the following factors directly influence the atmospheric dispersion of pollutants?

    b. Atmospheric stability

    d. Emission height

Correct answer(s):

    b. Atmospheric stability

Question 12:

Which of the following statements about ozone-depleting substances (ODSs) are
correct?

      a. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are ODSs.
      b. ODSs are highly reactive in the troposphere.
      c. Halons are ODSs.
      d. ODSs contribute to the destruction of stratospheric ozone.

Correct answer(s):
     a. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are ODSs
     c. Halons are ODSs
     d. ODSs contribute to the destruction of stratospheric ozone.
Solution: Refer to Slide #10 of Lecture #5. CFCs and halons are well-known ODSs. After
reaching the stratosphere, they release chlorine and bromine radicals that catalytically
destroy ozone. Most ODSs are actually chemically stable in the troposphere, which
allows them to persist long enough to reach the stratosphere. Therefore, statement b is
incorrect, while a, c, and d are correct.

Question 15:
A pollutant is emitted at the same rate from two stacks:

    · Stack A: 80 m height
    · Stack B: 250 m height

An atmospheric temperature inversion extends from the ground surface up to 200 m.
Which of the following statements are most likely correct?

    a. Pollutants from Stack A are more likely to be trapped below the inversion.
    b. Pollutants from Stack B may disperse more effectively.
    c. Pollutants from Stack B are more likely to be trapped below the inversion.
    d. Pollutants from Stack A may disperse more effectively.

Correct answer(s):
    a. Pollutants from Stack A are more likely to be trapped below the inversion.
    b. Pollutants from Stack B may disperse more effectively.

Solution: Refer to Slide #18 of Lecture #2.

The inversion layer extends from the surface to 200 m, so pollutants released below 200
m tend to remain trapped near the ground.

    · Stack A = 80 m: It releases pollutants within/below the inversion layer, so
         pollutants are more likely to be trapped. Hence, a is correct.

    · Stack B = 250 m: It releases pollutants above the inversion layer, so pollutants
         may disperse more effectively. Hence, b is correct.

Therefore, c and d are incorrect.
