When we talk about soil formation or pedogenesis, what are the factors that affect soil
formation? We will start with climate first. The first factor is rainfall. There are two parts
to this particular factor, leaching of minerals. So when rain falls on any soil or any
surface of the terrestrial environment, you will get dissolution of the minerals. So there is
loss of minerals or mass from the soil because of rainfall. You will also get wet
deposition of particulate matter and minerals from the atmosphere. So, any particulate
matter and this is something that is very easy to observe, you will find that whenever
there is a rain event, the atmosphere seems to be cleansed, the haze, the dust in the
atmosphere has been removed by the rainfall. So that is an example of wet deposition of
particulate matter and minerals from the atmosphere. So there is a net gain in terms of
mass of the soil. That is why I have two words mentioned here. Leaching of minerals is
net loss and wet deposition is net gain. What about temperature? You know that on a
daily basis, there is heating and cooling of the soil because of daytime and nighttime
temperatures. Now, this heating and cooling is going to result in contraction and
expansion of the soil material. So you have physical weathering of the material on a daily
basis. That is one role that temperature plays in pedogenesis. And the second point is
chemical weathering. When you have acidic rainwater, it will cause greater dissolution of
minerals. You know that acids result in the dissolution of minerals and therefore they will
break down whatever material is there, whether it is small particles or rocks, all of them
will be broken down depending on the acid content of rainwater. And also keep in mind
that in today's world, rainwater has a pH of around 5.6. Only because of CO2, it is an
equilibrium with CO2 in the atmosphere. So it is already acidic and if there is pollution in
the air to a greater extent, if you have NOX and SOX, then you will have nitric acid and
sulfuric acid, which will bring the pH of rainwater down further. So, depending on the
level of pollution in the environment, you will have some amounts of acids in rainwater
which will cause breakdown of the soil material.

One issue that I have already mentioned is soil erosion. So, we are going to cover the
universal soil loss equation. We will be looking at that in the subsequent slides and why
does soil erosion happen? It is basically a natural phenomenon. It is something that
happens due to wind, rain, temperature variations. All of this will cause soil erosion. We
can accelerate, human activities can accelerate soil erosion because of deforestation. So if
you are removing the rainforest, if you are removing other types of forest, that thick soil
layer which is forming naturally is going to be lost because there is no vegetation cover.
Overgrazing results in the same kinds of issues because when you have too many animals
grazing on the same area, what will eventually happen is that there is no time for the soil
to rejuvenate itself and for the vegetation to regrow. So you end up having denuded soil,
and this denuded soil has very less cohesive properties. So, during a rain event, it is very
easy for the soil to be washed away. One of the other factors is change in land use and
land cover. So because of change in land use and land cover, we also have major
problems with soil erosion. Because of deforestation, because of less vegetation cover,
we also have greater dust storms. Now, these dust storms happen for many reasons. One
is loss of vegetation, two is ploughing of the fields and three is drought conditions. All
these conditions lead to drying up of the top layer of soil and when the wind blows, all of
this becomes dust, and it is carried away. So soil erosion has several reasons for soil
erosion to happen.
Then we come to soil salinity. Soil salinity I have already mentioned is mainly due to
excessive use of water for irrigation and when this water evaporates, it leaves the salts
behind and you have high salt content in the soil. Now some of you may have observed
it, sometimes the soil has a whitish texture. It is visible to the eye, and that is because of
accumulation of salt on the surface of the soil. Groundwater irrigation is becoming more
and more popular. Groundwater irrigation has a major problem associated with it, and
that is groundwater generally contains more salt or higher TDS, total dissolved solids in
groundwater are generally higher than surface water. And if you have groundwater
irrigation, which is happening more and more frequently, it is going to lead to greater
salinization of soils and the end result is going to be reduced soil productivity in the long
term.
