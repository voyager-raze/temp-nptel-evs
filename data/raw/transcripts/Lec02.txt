So just imagine that you have a village with a bunch of herdsmen and each of these
herdsmen has only one sheep or goat and these sheep or goat are grazing in a pasture land
which is common, it is nobody's private property, but each sheep or goat is the property of
the herdsman, the individual herdsman. One particular herdsman decides that he can use
some of his savings to go and buy another sheep or goat and he does exactly that. He finds
that the benefit of having one additional sheep or goat is very high and therefore the utility
in mathematical terms is very high. So, he benefits enormously from having another one.
And when everyone realizes that he is benefiting so much they all follow in the same train of
thought and each one starts adding over a period of time, each one starts adding more and
more to his or her herd. In the example it is just a male, but regardless. So there then comes
a point in time and this is sort of an abstract way of thinking about what is the consequence
of limitless growth. So, each one of them is adding an infinite number of sheep or goat to
their herd. Eventually the pasture land that all the sheep and goat are being reared on is
going to be exhausted. It is not going to be able to replenish itself.
It is now at some point in time it is going to the number of sheep and goat are going to
exceed what is called the assimilative capacity of that particular environment. So, that is
what we call a destructive exploitative philosophy and that is the tragedy of the commons.
That each of us as individuals are utilizing natural resources that are common resources,
they are public property. They are nobody's private property just like our air, water, some
of the common lands that we use without being aware of the fact that we are benefiting
from them. So, that is the tragedy of the commons that we are looking at limitless growth.
We are not worried about the future. We are not thinking in terms of the long term. So, that
has been the course of human history for the last few centuries or even longer. What we
want is to shift from that type of thinking to one that allows us to recycle as much of our
natural resources that we are using and I do not want to use the word recycle. I want to say
that we conserve our resources as much as possible. We protect our environment and
thereby we also protect the inhabitants including ourselves as well as other species from
destruction. So, I will be talking a lot more about all these issues in the remaining lectures.
