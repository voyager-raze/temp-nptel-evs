You are all familiar with the hydrologic cycle, but just to make sure that every one of us
is completely clear about the terminology, I will start with that. So, we know that water
evaporates from both terrestrial as well as ocean water bodies. So you have terrestrial
water bodies which are not shown in the schematic, but you have oceans and seas and so
on. So, any surface water body is subject to evaporation. So evapotranspiration, the usage
of water by plants that is transpiration and evaporation is happening from any surface
water body. So that sends the water, it converts basically the water into water vapour and
that water vapour is accumulating in the atmosphere. When the level of water vapour in
the atmosphere becomes completely saturated, it starts condensing and it comes down as
precipitation. Now, when it comes down as precipitation, it comes down over both
terrestrial as well as marine environments. Now, this precipitation can be in any form, it
can be rain, it can be hail, it can be ice, snow and so many other forms that may happen.
So when this precipitation falls on ground surface, two things can happen to it. If the
ground surface, the subsurface is saturated, then you get surface runoff which means a
sheet of water is formed on the surface of the ground and all of that water will seek the
lowest possible level. So it will run towards any low-lying area or any low-lying surface
water body. So that surface water body can be rivers, streams, ponds, lakes or even the
ocean and sea. So that we call surface runoff. If the subsurface is not saturated, part of the
precipitation will go deeper into the subsurface because as long as it is not saturated,
there will be inflow of water from the surface into the subsurface and that we call
infiltration. Now this infiltrated water will eventually go to a groundwater aquifer. We
are not going to go into all those details, but this infiltration will add to the groundwater
resources. So this surface runoff is going to contribute water to surface water bodies and
the infiltrated water will contribute water to groundwater bodies. So groundwater is not
visible to the eye, it is there in the subsurface and these days we are becoming heavily
dependent on groundwater. I will be talking about that in a few slides after a few slides.

So, what we have in this slide is a representation a graphical representation of the
different types of pollution sources. Now, why is our water getting polluted? Now, within
these pollution sources, we can categorize them into categories. One is point sources and
the other is nonpoint sources. If you walk down any river bank, you will find that there
are drains that are discharging directly into the river. You might see the same kind of
scenario in lakes and ponds, where you have a pipe that is discharging wastewater
directly into the water body. So those are called point sources because it is a small pipe,
relatively small pipe that is discharging the water. So, it is a point source. Nonpoint
sources are where you have large areas that are contributing water to the water body or
the land and so on. So, whenever you see a Nala or a pipe that is discharging wastewater,
it can be municipal wastewater or it can be industrial wastewater. So, these residential
and commercial activities, industrial activities, all of these wastewaters are going to be
discharged generally through pipelines. And these pipelines are all point sources. So, you
can see it in this graphic over here. You have the wastewater treatment plant discharge
after treatment. Unfortunately in our country, not all wastewater is treated, some of it is
partially treated, some of it is completely untreated. So, we have industrial wastewater,
we have municipal wastewater and we have treated wastewater. All of these are
considered point sources. We then come to solid waste dumps. Now, solid waste dumps
or even landfills, they can be either municipal or they can be industrial and you have
leachate that comes out of these solid waste dumps. Some of these dumps occupy a small
area and the leachate may be a small little stream that is going directly into a water body.
In that case, it is considered a point source. But then there are certain areas that are huge.
They are occupying acres and acres of land are occupied by these solid waste dumps.
And you have multiple sources of leachate that are going into either the subsurface or
into the surface water bodies.

So, pollution in these cases happens because of leachate from solid waste dumps and it
pollutes the groundwater as well as the surface water. So, that is something that is not
mentioned in the slide. It is not shown over here, but that is also a major issue in many
parts of the world including our own country.
So, just the last part of it, when we think about nonpoint sources, we have surface runoff
from urban areas as well as rural areas. In urban areas, we have paved areas. These paved
areas include roads, pavements, parking lots as well as the built-up area. Now, when
precipitation falls on these paved areas, it takes with it the dust, soil, and chemicals that
are basically lying on the dry paved areas and then they are picked up by the
precipitation. These dry materials come from traffic emissions from commercial and
industrial activities. And when there is a storms on rainstorms and so on, all this ends up
in the surface water body as surface runoff. So, these are considered nonpoint sources.
In rural areas, we have agricultural fields and feed lots. Now these agricultural fields and
feed lots have, you have pesticide use, you have fertilizer use, you have so many other
chemicals that are being used these days and you also have wastewater coming from the
feed lots. So, from rural areas, the surface runoff is polluted by these chemicals and these
chemicals will eventually end up in the surface water bodies as well as groundwater if it
infiltrates into the groundwater.

So now we come to one of the last concepts which is a relatively new concept and that is
environmental flows. Now, it is standard practice now to maintain environmental flows in
perennial rivers. This is a requirement for all major and minor perennial rivers and the
reason is that we do not want these rivers to lose their water, to lose any or rather to put it
in another way, no section of these rivers should be completely dry. What is happening in
certain parts of the world including our own is that water from these perennial rivers is
being diverted for irrigation. We have already seen that irrigation is the biggest water
user. So, if all, not just 90 percent but if almost 100 percent of the water is diverted to
canals for irrigation, there will be nothing left in the river for the aquatic ecosystems to
survive. So, e-flows is now the norm, it has to be followed to ensure that all rivers
continue to have healthy aquatic ecosystems and all of the water is not diverted for
irrigation. So, to maintain the flora and fauna at a healthy level in all these aquatic
ecosystems, especially in riverine ecosystems, it is now essential to maintain what is
called environmental flow. So, this environmental flow is the minimum flow required to
be maintained throughout the length of the river.
