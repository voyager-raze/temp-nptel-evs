So, we will now come to the last topic of this week and that is nutrient cycling.
Nutrients are cycled through the natural environment through what we call the
biogeochemical cycles. The reason they are called biogeochemical cycles is that they
involve biological, geological and chemical reactions and processes for the recycling of
these environments in the natural environment. I am going to start with plants and
remember that we are looking at soil. We are interested in understanding how soil pollution
can happen and what are the consequences. So, these biogeochemical cycles are actually
the cycling of the nutrients through all domains of the atmosphere of the environment.

So, all the four domains of the environment include atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere
and biosphere. And soil is one of the subsets of the lithosphere. So, any disturbance to the
nutrient cycling in soil will cause a disturbance in the biogeochemical cycling of the most
important nutrients. Now, what are those most important nutrients? There are 16 elements
that plants cannot grow without. These 16 elements are grouped into two categories, one
is macronutrients and the other is micronutrients.

Macronutrients are required in large amounts and micronutrients are required in trace
amounts, trace amounts meaning very small amounts. The macronutrients include carbon,
hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, sulphur, calcium, magnesium, potassium and
chloride. Micronutrients include iron, manganese, zinc, copper, boron and molybdenum.
Carbon, hydrogen and oxygen are present in the atmosphere and they are present in large
amounts. They are generally not growth limiting factors and currently carbon in the
atmosphere is increasing.

So, it is definitely not a growth limiting factor but because it is the basis of life, we will be
looking at the carbon cycle as well. What is growth limiting factor? What is the meaning
of the term growth limiting factor? It comes from the law of the minimum. The law of the
minimum states that every organism on the planet has a particular set of requirements for
all the important growth elements. These are all the important growth elements for plants.
For other organisms there can be small differences, but the bulk of it is more or less the
same.

Macronutrients are more or less the same. The micronutrients can differ from one
organism to another. Coming back to the law of the minimum, the law of the minimum
states that every organism has a particular set of requirements for each element and the
element that is available, that is least available relative to the requirements of that organism
is going to limit the growth of that organism. So, that organism will grow in proportion to
the availability of the growth limiting factor and the factor is a nutrient. We use the term
growth limiting factor or you can also say growth limiting nutrient. So, when you think
about nutritional deficiencies, when you think about growth limiting concentrations, that
is what we are looking at.
We are looking at these elements that can limit the growth of any particular organism. So,
the growth limiting factors in agricultural productivity and for plants, they are generally
either nitrogen, phosphorus or sulphur and these are the four biogeochemical cycles that
we are going to look at carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and sulphur.
