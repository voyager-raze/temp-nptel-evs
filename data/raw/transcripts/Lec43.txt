Then we have ultimate analysis. I would not go into too many details. Ultimate analysis

Then we have organic carbon. Organic carbon also requires a fairly sophisticated
instrument called the TOC analyzer. TOC stands for total organic carbon and you can
take a solid sample, you cannot inject it, you can place it in the instrument and it gets
combusted at very high temperature and based on a calibration, you can determine the
organic carbon percentage in the entire sample. And the last point is calorific value. I will

So, we will start with moisture content. Samples of 100 g can be taken and all
measurements should be ideally done in triplicate. Once you take this sample in a
crucible, you dry it to a constant weight in an oven at 105  for 24 hours, minimum 24
hours. But what we often find is that if the sample is really wet, you will have to perhaps
dry it for a longer period of time and you take measurements at hourly intervals when you
start getting the same weight, there is no further change in weight, then that is considered
the final reading. You cool the sample in a desiccator and record the difference in the
weight, the initial weight and the final weight.

So, the weight lost during the process of drying is called moisture content. Then we
come to calorific value. For determining calorific value, we use an instrument called a
bomb calorimeter. It is a very simple instrument, it is got a little bomb, it is literally called
a bomb and it has two hemispheres into which a small amount of sample is placed. It is
then combusted within that bomb device and because of the combustion, the water
temperature in the calorimeter increases.

There are equations which are there in the textbooks and so on and the difference in
temperature will allow you to determine the gross as well as net calorific value at
constant volume. This is very important when we think about using different fractions of
municipal solid waste for energy generation. Then we come to total solids, volatile solids
and fixed solids. We then look at total solids, volatile solids and fixed solids. You can use
the same methods.

These are standard methods that we use for a whole range of samples. You can use it for
sediment, you can use it for sludge and you can use it for municipal solid waste or any
other type of solid waste. So these are solid or semi-solid samples from either water or
waste water treatment plants or they can be even from municipal solid waste. Like I said
any solid material is, any solid material can be analyzed using these methods. So total
solids are what is left in the sample after the moisture has been removed.

So total solids are defined as the solids left in the sample after it has been dried at 105 .
You can take these total solids after drying and put them in what is called a muffle
furnace. The muffle furnace has a much higher temperature. It goes up to 550 . It goes
far beyond 550 but you are supposed to set it at 550  and after the entire sample has
been combusted you can take it out and take the difference of the initial weight and the
final weight.

The fraction of solids that is remaining is defined as fixed solids and the material that gets

Now the municipality's responsibility begins with collection and the bulk of the budget
of any urban local body in India is basically spent on collection. It is an expensive
activity, it requires transportation in these specialized vehicles, it requires two to five
member teams to go around and pick up the waste. So it is labor and equipment intensive
and 50 to 70% of the solid waste management budget is spent on collection alone.
Because they are short of funds, financial resources and technical resources in some cases
because most of the municipalities have very little equipment for doing the remaining
activities basically for segregation of mixed waste, for removing recyclables from mixed
waste and for treatment and proper disposal.
