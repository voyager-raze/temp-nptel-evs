In this lecture we are going to look at some important judgments passed by NGT or the
National Green Tribunal.
Just to remind you that the National Green Tribunal was set up in 2010 and we will be
looking at some of the major initiatives as well as judgments of the NGT since then.

Keywords for this particular lecture are solid waste management, Al Mitra Patel, Samir

We will start with one of the most famous environmental cases that started in the Supreme
Court because at that time the National Green Tribunal did not exist. So, this particular
public interest litigation something that I can mention over here is most of the
environmental cases that go to court are filed as public interest litigation because of the
nature of the issues. So, way back in 1996 when the National Green Tribunal did not exist,
Almitra Patel who is an environmentalist and an activist along with others filed a public
interest litigation under article 32 of the constitution of India in the Supreme Court. He
sought immediate and urgent improvement in the practices adopted in the treatment of
municipal solid waste or garbage in India in four major metropolitan cities. So, our four
major cities Delhi, Bombay, Chennai and Kolkata. The Supreme Court of India decided to
hear the petition for the National Capital Territory of Delhi only. In 1997 Supreme Court
of India in response to this litigation asked every state pollution control board or rather
every state which eventually went to the state pollution control board to provide reports on
the level of compliance in with respect to solid waste management under the existing rules.
The outcome was that almost everywhere non-compliance was found. In fact, there was no
compliance at that point in time. Noticing the fact that there was complete non-compliance
in the entire country when it came to urban or municipal solid waste management, the
Supreme Court further ordered the formation of what is called the Aseem Barman
committee in 1998. Almitra Patel was also a member of this committee and she was, she
runs her own non-governmental organization for improving municipal solid waste
management services in many places. So, what was the situation in Delhi at that time? In
Delhi garbage was strewn all over the place, it was typical and this was not just in Delhi.
The whole idea of using Delhi as an example because it is the national capital and because
the national capital itself was so poorly maintained and so severely polluted, If you read
the judgment, it is a very interesting way for the Supreme Court to talk about how bad the
situation was in the national capital and if the situation was bad in the national capital, we
know how bad it was everywhere else as well. So, the municipal government of Delhi was
not paying enough attention to the problem of not just hazardous waste management, but
also municipal solid waste management. Many ailments, respiratory problems, skin
disorders afflicted the Delhi population. Trash from factories was being openly dumped
into the Yamuna River. The Yamuna River is the drinking water source for the entire city
of Delhi. These wastes were contaminated. The Delhi municipal administration had failed
to properly treat and handle these wastes. The Supreme Court passed its judgment on 15th
of February 2000 and in 2014, it transferred all issues related to solid and liquid waste
management to the National Green Tribunal.

So, the judgment of the National Green Tribunal after this case was transferred to it, the
highlights are shown in this particular slide. It was common practice in the country for
burning solid waste. Municipal solid waste was very often burned and it resulted in toxic
fumes for the entire environment around that particular open dump site. So, part of the
national, the complete prohibition of open burning of waste on land including landfills was
one of the most important directions of the NGT in this particular case. Every state and
union territory were directed by the tribunal to immediately enforce the solid waste
management rules of 2000. So, remember that when this particular case went to the
Supreme Court, that was in 1996. In 1996, there were no formal solid waste management
rules at all in the country. Those rules came in 2000. So, after 2000, we had some rules and
NGT itself was established in 2010. So, at that point, they asked every state and union
territory to enforce the rules. They instructed the central government, state government, all
local bodies and all citizens to fulfill their respective obligations under these rules. A
guideline was released to ensure adequate segregation of waste prior to the production of
the waste. A guideline was particularly for thermal power plants. Now thermal power
plants as I mentioned in a previous lecture are major sources of fly ash. This fly ash has to
be segregated from all other waste. So, this was another major point. It also mandated the
provision of buffer zones around the power plants and landfills. Expected segregation of
waste was made compulsory for energy plants, basically thermal power plants and landfills.
They cannot be used for the deposition of biodegradable waste. They can be used only for
the deposition of inert waste and whatever biodegradable material does end up in the waste,
it should be subject to biostabilization within six months. The fact of the matter is even
today when you look at the open dump sites in the country, this particular point is still not
being complied with.

In another important judgment by the National Green Tribunal, a group of citizens in the
Uttarakhand region formed a samiti, the Srinagar Bandh Aapda Sangarsh Samiti was
formed and they took the Alaknanda hydropower project or the company that was running
the project to court. What you can see in this photo, this is a NASA satellite image of the
northern Indian subcontinent with monsoon clouds over the state of Uttarakhand, Himachal
and Uttar Pradesh. So, this is the state of the monsoon clouds that brought in, there was a
cloud burst, many of you may be remembering that, that in 2013 there was a huge cloud
burst over this entire region. It ended up killing more than 6000 people and more than
60,000 tourists and pilgrims were either injured or stranded in this region because of
torrential rains. The petitioners in this case sought compensation from the hydroelectric
power company asking for about 9.3 crores against damage suffered by the petitioners in
terms of life and property. The claim was the first respondent had dumped a huge quantity
of muck. Muck was basically sediment, silt and so on that was generated during the
construction of the Alaknanda hydroelectric project. Because of heavy rains which
happened due to the cloud burst that everyone knows about, the reservoir of the project
was filled up completely, the dam gates were opened and this silt and sediment eventually
flowed into the villages resulting in huge loss of life and property for the members of the
Samiti. The National Green Tribunal had to decide whether the respondent, in this case the
Alaknanda hydro power electric company was liable to pay the claimed compensation.

So, this was their judgment. The tribunal reached the conclusion that damage to the
property as alleged by the applicants was incurred because of flood waters and the flood
waters brought with them soil, sediment, muck and so many other problems. All this
material entered the residential premises. There was a material called Phyllite. Phyllite is a
product generated during the digging of the tunnel and the canal and because of the
excavation of the powerhouse downstream of the barrage. So according to NGT, the
contribution of the project to the problems associated with this natural disaster were clear.
So even though the basis was a natural disaster, the contribution of the hydroelectric power
company to exacerbating the damage was clear. So, the tribunal noted that although the
2013 Uttarakhand floods were a result of the cloud burst, the damage that was caused in
the residential area was not an act of God. The muck from the project was about 30% and
that was the footprint of respondent 1 which means the Alaknanda hydroelectric power
project. Even if the idea of the act of God was to be invoked, the tribunal saw that the no
fault liability principle was used in this case and that meant respondent 1 which means the
Alaknanda hydroelectric power company was liable to pay part of the compensation which
was about rupees 1 lakh to each of the applicants along with their cost. This is one of the
major judgments where NGT directly relied on the principle of polluter pays and a private
entity was held liable to pay compensation making this a landmark judgment. So, like I
said it was a landmark judgment because even though the entire thing was a natural disaster
it was exacerbated because of this private company.

Then we come to another interesting case and this was Sameer Mehta versus the Union of
India and others. This case involved questions of public importance and significant
environmental jurisprudence. On 4th of August 2011 a merchant ship, a merchant vehicle
called MV Rakh sank. You can see a photo over here; it sank 20 nautical miles outside off
of the coast of Mumbai. India's territorial waters extend up to 12 nautical miles. The ship
was owned and managed by a Qatar based company and was carrying approximately
60,000 tons of coal, 290 tons of fuel oil and 50 tons of diesel oil. In the photo you can see
the slick from the sinking of the ship. This brown area around the ship is the oil slick. The
ship's cargo was coming from Indonesia and it was to be delivered to Dahej in Gujarat. The
oil slick was sighted on August 6th. It was spreading steadily and going towards the coast.
The Indian coast guard had two ships spraying the slick with dispersants and even then, the
slick was still coming close to the coast. A thick film of oil was formed on the surface of
the sea and large-scale damage was caused to mangroves and the marine ecosystem. An
environmentalist Samir Mehta filed an application with the NGT asking for damages or
compensation caused due to the sinking of this ship.
This case was filed in front of the National Green Tribunal. The judgment of the NGT was
that the respondents in this case were highly negligent in their duties. So, negligence could
be attributed to many of the respondents. There is a long list of respondents. Like I said
please refer to the original judgments for details. The said respondents, these respondents
5, 6, 7 and 11 had not adhered to the principles of pre-voyage due diligence despite having
sufficient time to do so. The sinking of the ship was a result of the negligence of the
respondents, meaning the owners of the ship, those managing it, those operating the
shipping line and the people providing the cargo as well as those receiving the cargo. All
were considered respondents in this case. The sinking of the ship was a result of the
negligence of the respondents and the NGT upheld the principle of polluter pays and
directed respondents 5, 7 and 11 to pay environmental composition to the tune of 100 crore
rupees to the Ministry of Shipping, which is the biggest compensation amount ever paid
by a private entity against environmental damage. Respondent 6 was asked to pay for the
compensation of 5 crores.
We now come to another interesting case and this is in the Arunachal region. So, the
Tawang region of Arunachal Pradesh is where you have what is called the Mon region.
Now in this Mon region, we have black necked cranes. These black necked cranes are
considered an endangered species. They are classified as vulnerable and the Mon tribe that
lives in this region considers them to be sacred. In fact, they consider them to be
reincarnations of the Dalai Lama or something like that. So an appeal was filed by an
organisation named the Save Mon region Federation along with a social activist against the
grant of environmental clearance given to a 6400 crore hydroelectric power project, which
was proposed in this particular region. This project is situated close to the winter site of
this black necked grain is a migratory bird and this particular region is the winter site of
these migratory birds. They are part of the IUCN red list of endangered species and they
are classified as vulnerable. This area is also home to several other endangered species like
the snow leopard, red panda, Arunachal macaque, another photo is shown over here.
So, what was the judgment? Many of the points here are quoted directly from the judgment
of the NGT. The environmental clearance, which was dated 19th April, 2011 was
suspended until the time that the following studies were carried out, their public
consultation had to be done. The Public Support Appraisal Committee had to consider the
outcome of this public consultation. They had to reappraise the proposal for the grant of
environmental clearance, make recommendations to the Ministry of Environmental Forests
and Climate Change and the Ministry would then act upon the recommendations according
to the law. The Ministry would also set up another study for the E-FLOW requirements for
protection of the habitat of the black necked crane and for conservation of the black necked
crane through the Wildlife Institution of India in Dehradun. This would be done as soon as
possible. The study report along with another study of the Tawang river basin would be
available for public consultation in compliance with the Environment Protection Act. The
EAC would then act on these recommendations and they would go through a full appraisal
of the proposal all over again for grant of environmental clearance and the appropriate
decision would be made, appropriate decision based on the recommendations would be
provided to the Ministry who would then take the final decision. The appeal was disposed
of with no orders about the cost sharing of this work.
What you see on this slide are some NGT orders in the year 2022. It is a summary from a
particular newspaper, the business standard. So, what they did is they summarized some of
the landmark orders of NGT just for 2022. It gives us an idea about what the NGT has been
dealing with and what are the nature of the orders that they have been putting out. So, the
NGT has been taking initiatives to monitor compliance with environmental regulations by
states as well as union territories. The two authorities that have not adhered to the
environmental rules and regulations have been directed to pay compensation for damage
to human health and property in many cases. So here we have a list of the states
Maharashtra, Telangana, West Bengal, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Jammu,
Kashmir. All these states were found to be non-compliant with the solid waste management
rules of 2016. They have been asked to pay compensation to the affected people wherever
possible. So, some of the compensation amounts are extremely high. Another more specific
case is NGT held the municipal corporation of Ludhiana responsible for the death of seven
people that happened due to a fire near a Ludhiana dump site in April 2022. The cause of
the accident was improper handling of 30 lakh metric tons of municipal solid waste. Now
when you have open dumping of waste, it is not segregated, it has both biodegradable and
non-biodegradable materials and because of that you get hazardous gases including
methane and also many other contaminants which can pollute the groundwater. So, a
penalty of Rs 100 crores was imposed on the municipal corporation of Ludhiana by NGT.
In Delhi, there are several dump sites, there are several extremely large dump sites,
Bhalswa is one of them. They have 1 crore metric tons of municipal solid waste which have
had recurring fires. Now these recurring fires are obviously indicators of the failure in
handling this material. So NGT highlighted that and observed that citizens cannot be
allowed to face emergency situations because of lack of governance. It directed the Delhi
government to pay Rs 900 crores as environmental compensation for improper
management of municipal solid waste.

There are many other orders. For example, illegal sand mining has been a major issue all
over the country. The tribunal has issued orders for carrying out drone-based surveys and
effectively prohibiting illegal sand mining activities. They also have imposed costs as well
as compensation for damages cost. They have also issued directions to prevent illegal
mining of stones and granite that are conducted without environmental clearance or proper
safety norms. NGT has also imposed environmental compensation and prohibitive orders
against construction companies for extracting groundwater without permission. In case of
road construction and development projects where felling of trees and encroachment in
wildlife sanctuaries are noticed, NGT directed redesigning of the projects and ordered strict
adherence to the laws of environmental clearance and formulation of plantation and
rehabilitation plans. A music festival was proposed near the Ranthambore Tiger Reserve
in Rajasthan. NGT ruled that permission can only be given subject to the conditions
imposed by a joint committee comprising the National Wildlife Board and the state's chief
wildlife warden. On the issue of illegal functioning of hotels, resorts, pubs, clubs, ashrams
and other commercial activities in the Uttarakhand region, we have the Chilla range of the
Rajaji National Park in Uttarakhand. On this particular issue, the tribunal directed all
operating establishments to be closed and all commercial activity in the forest has to be
stopped.
There are some more highlights over here. Environmental clearance that was granted to a
private builder for the construction of a group housing project in the national capital near
the Vishwa Vidyalaya metro station was set aside. For matters pertaining to industrial
pollution, the tribunal directed the ministry to review and streamline the functioning of
state environmental impact assessment authorities. The tribunal has also passed orders
based on complaints of deteriorating ambient air quality due to the operation of brick kilns,
stone crushers and hot mix plants. While hearing a petition about indoor air quality
standards, the tribunal ordered a committee with officials drawn from the ministry and
CPCB to develop an appropriate mechanism in coordination with other ministries like the
Ministry of Urban Affairs and the Ministry of Health. The NGT also has said that brick
kilns in the national capital region can operate only after assessing the carrying capacity of
air in a particular area while following the notifications of the ministry and the directives
of the Supreme Court.
Some of the other initiatives that the NGT has been monitoring, first thing is rooftop
rainwater harvesting. They have been monitoring that. They have been monitoring the
water quality in all rivers in the country with a goal to achieve bathing water quality in all
rivers in the country. They have also been monitoring and ensuring compliance with the
solid waste management rules of 2016 at all Indian railway stations. One of the other major
initiatives that has started recently is the removal, treatment, disposal and resource recovery
from legacy municipal solid waste all over the country. So, whenever you see open dump
sites, you may also see in the near future if it is not already happening that these dump sites
are now being cleaned and cleared and the material is now being treated and resources
recovered from it for proper for recycling as well as proper disposal.
