I will end this lecture with one definition, one set of definitions and that is what is the
difference between a pollutant or a contaminant. These two words are used very frequently
and sometimes we think of them as synonymous and they are not really synonymous in their
meaning, but they do have overlapping meaning. So let us take a look at that. What is a
pollutant? A pollutant is a chemical species that causes undesirable effects on the
environment or any of its components. It does not have to be anthropogenic in origin, it can
be natural in origin. The keyword is undesirable. Is it causing any adverse biological effect?
So, a simpler definition of pollutant is that any chemical or element that causes adverse
biological effect on the resident community, that resident community may be human beings
or other organisms. So as long as it is an adverse biological effect that chemical becomes a
pollutant. So that is the simplest way of identifying whether a particular chemical is a
pollutant or not. These undesirable effects like I said are primarily endangering the health of
humans and other organisms. The broader definition includes endangering the safety as well
as causing financial and aesthetic losses. So, you can see one is slightly narrow in terms of its
definition, in terms of its biological effect definition and there are other people who use the
word more broadly for anything that causes financial and aesthetic losses as well.

Coming to the second word and that is contaminant or contamination. What is
contamination? Contamination is the presence of a substance where it should not be or it may
be at concentrations above the background level. So let us, the best way to understand this is
by taking examples. Let us start with pollutant. I have already said a pollutant can be natural
or anthropogenic. So, if I think about arsenic or fluoride in groundwater, these are natural
pollutants. Both of them are detrimental or adverse. They have adverse biological effects on
human beings if they are present at fairly, even though they may be present at fairly low
concentrations, they can still have adverse biological effects on the residential community of
human beings basically. Now they are natural pollutants. We can also have anthropogenic
pollutants, we have synthetic organic compounds like DDT, like so many other pesticides that
are present and we use them in our environment on a daily basis. So those are anthropogenic
pollutants. Let us now come to contamination. What is contamination? Let us imagine that
we have a river and that river, in fact all water bodies have some amount of natural organic
matter. This comes from natural sources. It is basically coming from the decay of vegetation.
And it is considered non-toxic. It is not considered to have any adverse biological effect. So, it
is not pollution, but it is contamination under certain conditions. And let me finish this. Let
me elaborate a little more on this. So when we discharge municipal wastewater into a river,
we are also adding more natural organic matter. If we exceed the assimilative capacity of the
river, we create what are called nuisance conditions. So, it is natural organic matter, but it is
at concentrations much higher than the background. And because the concentrations are
much higher than the background, they exceed the assimilative capacity of the river and
therefore nuisance conditions are created. So that is contamination. Now we can have
situations where you have a particular chemical that is both pollutant as well as contaminant.
So, you can see that the two definitions are very different and, in some cases, they overlap
and in other cases they do not overlap.
